The Takeaway

Today's feed is about what changes once the agent is the surface. Peter Yang asks it from the business side — "what happens when agents browse your website and get the job done without a human seeing your ads at all?" — and describes it from the user side in the same hour: "I no longer live in email or text, I live in the chat with my agent(s)." Neither post carries a number. That is the substance of the claim: the human is no longer in the loop where the money was being made, and nobody in the feed disputes it.

Aaron Levie gives the same shift its structural and commercial reading, and his two posts are the only place today where the argument is complete. Agents "will use software 100X more than people ever did", which makes the primitives more load-bearing rather than less, because agents "can take destructive actions in our systems" — security and guardrails, data management, workflow orchestration are the three jobs he names. And the spend follows the delegation: start with the annoying tasks, end with more spend through these systems than before. He splits the prize between the agent providers and whoever builds the layer the agents transact against. Worth noting the number is the one he used on 09-16, moved from volume of work to volume of software used — the second use is the harder one to falsify.

The counterweight is about restraint, and it comes from two directions. Nikunj: the companies "boasting about tokenmaxxing have the worst product experiences", because good products are "curation & gardening" rather than throwing everything at an agent. Thariq, in one line: "use big pictures and few words", several times a day — steering by constraint, repeatedly, instead of specifying once. Set against Levie's expansion, that is the day's real tension. The same people expecting agents to absorb 100X more software are also the ones saying the product work is deciding what not to hand them.

Three threads from earlier issues moved today. Jev went from a benchmark claim to something addressable — promised for Vercel AI Gateway on 09-18, now "over HTTP in AI Gateway", with the Latent Space episode Swyx announced yesterday out this morning; six days from claim to distribution, and still no independent benchmark in this feed. Two separate hints point at an imminent OpenAI consumer agent: Sottiaux's "I promised a reset for Tuesday", and Nikunj's "reportedly get ready to release their 'agent' to the masses", which arrives with the ask that they "showcase the work". And Steinberger's correction — Meta built its own agent, "being inspired" — lands one day after Peter Yang ranked Meta's Muse first on product and promotion.

核心结论

今天这份 feed 讲的是：当 agent 成为界面之后，什么会变。Peter Yang 从生意那一侧问出来——"当 agent 浏览你的网站、把事情办完，而全程没有一个人类看到你的广告时，会发生什么？"——又在同一小时里从用户那一侧描述它："我不再活在邮件和短信里了，我活在我和我的 agent(s) 的对话里。"两条都没有数字。数字缺失本身就是主张的实质：人已经不在那个赚钱的环节里了，而 feed 里没有人反驳这一点。

Aaron Levie 给同一个位移做了结构侧和商业侧的解读，他的两条也是今天唯一把论证讲完整的地方。Agent"使用软件的量会是人类的一百倍"，这让底层原语承得更重而不是更轻，因为 agent"能在我们的系统里执行破坏性操作"——他点出的三件事是安全与护栏、数据管理、工作流编排。而支出会跟着委托走：从烦人的小任务开始，最后通过这些系统花的钱比之前更多。他把这份好处劈成两半，一半给 agent 提供方，一半给任何去建 agent 要与之交易那一层的人。值得注意的是这个数字正是他 09-16 用过的那个，从"工作量"挪到了"软件使用量"——第二次这个用法更难证伪。

反方向的力量来自克制，而且来自两个方向。Nikunj：那些"吹嘘 tokenmaxxing 的公司，产品体验是最差的"，因为好产品靠的是"策展和打理"，不是把所有东西都丢给 agent。Thariq 用一句话："用大图、少写字"，一天好几次——用约束去反复引导，而不是一次性把规格写满。把它和 Levie 的扩张放在一起，就是今天真正的张力所在：正是这批期待 agent 吞下 100 倍软件的人，同时在说产品工作其实在于决定哪些东西不要交给它们。

前几期的三条线今天都有推进。Jev 从一个基准测试主张变成了可以被调用的东西——09-18 承诺会进 Vercel AI Gateway，今天是"可以在 AI Gateway 里通过 HTTP 使用"，而 Swyx 昨天预告的那期 Latent Space 节目今早已经上线；从主张到分发用了六天，这份 feed 里仍然没有出现独立的基准测试。两条分开的线索同时指向一个即将到来的 OpenAI 消费级 agent：Sottiaux 的"我答应过周二会有一次 reset"，和 Nikunj 的"据报道正准备把他们的'agent'推向大众"，后者还附了一句要求——多"展示做出来的东西"。而 Steinberger 那句更正——Meta 是自己做的 agent，"受了启发"——恰好落在 Peter Yang 把 Meta 的 Muse 排在产品与推广第一位的第二天。
