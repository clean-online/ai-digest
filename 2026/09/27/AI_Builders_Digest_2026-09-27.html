Guillermo Rauch Vercel CEO

Guillermo Rauch writes the clearest statement yet of what an agentic deployment platform means in practice, using Klaviyo as the example: “① Connect every agent (claude, codex, cursor…) ② Configure SSO via their IDP (Okta, Entra) ③ Now everyone can cook, securely®”. Then he asks the question that follows from it — “What happens to SaaS once every company sets this up?” — and answers it in three moves. The data has to come from somewhere, which is why “we see the big enterprise SaaS players suddenly prioritize shipping CLIs and MCPs. Or dust off APIs they were neglecting for years.” The buying criterion changes: “The new 'procurement bar' will be how ergonomic your product is for *agents*, rather than humans. How easily they can navigate your ontology and work with your data.” And the tail goes away: “there's a long tail of SaaS applications that I suspect will never be bought again. They'll be generated. They'll be more secure, more performant, more modern, and tailored to each company's and employee's needs.”

Guillermo Rauch 用 Klaviyo 作例子，写下了到目前为止最清楚的一段话，说明 agentic deployment platform 在实践中是什么：“① 连接每一个 agent（claude、codex、cursor……）② 通过他们的 IDP（Okta、Entra）配置 SSO ③ 现在所有人都可以安全地下厨了®”。然后他追问了由此而来的那个问题 —— “一旦每家公司都这么搭好了，SaaS 会怎样？” —— 并分三步回答。数据总得从某处来，这就是为什么“我们看到大型企业级 SaaS 玩家突然开始优先交付 CLI 和 MCP，或者把他们荒废多年的 API 重新擦亮”。采购标准变了：“新的‘采购门槛’将是你的产品对 agent 来说有多顺手，而不是对人类来说。它们能多容易地 navigating 你的 ontology、处理你的数据。”而长尾消失了：“有一长串 SaaS 应用我怀疑再也不会被买第二次。它们会被生成出来。它们会更安全、更高性能、更现代，并且按每家公司、每个员工的需要定制。”

His second post is the compressed version of the same shift — “We used to write code, now we write English” — attached to the growth of skills: “I watched it grow from idea, to first ship, to npx skills being on every README of the internet.” What is worth noting about both posts is which axis they sit on: they are claims about adoption, procurement and distribution, not measurements of what the models can do. His third post is a two-word reaction with a link and nothing else.

Re-Founding Incumbents for the AI Era with Sequence Holdings Co-Founder and CEO Michael Lee
The Takeaway Michael Lee's argument is that the binding constraint on applying AI to an existing business is not the model, it is who the organisation celebrates. “Every company on the planet has a celebrated persona… In a world where you believe that alpha comes from engineering and AI, you need to create a culture whereby the celebrated persona is the engineer.” From there he rules out the two routes a Fortune 500 CEO can actually take. Services firms optimise for “getting in your wallet, staying in your wallet, growing the share of your wallet. It is a path towards incrementalism.” Software vendors have to sell to “a workflow as it's designed today, as humans are set up in that human assembly line today,” because “you can't sell a product around a new human assembly line that doesn't exist today.” What is left is owning the company — a permanent holding company doing “one deal per year,” with the duration of its capital matched to a transformation he measures in decades. The reason the hour lands rather than floats is BankSouth: consumer underwriting time down 94% since March, commercial loans from 30 days end to end to 11 days, and a Q2 in which loan volumes doubled against Q1 with a smaller underwriting team. He volunteers the caveat on that last number himself: “This is purely luck. I'm not going to say sequence had anything to do with this.”
核心结论 Michael Lee 的论点是：把 AI 用到一门现成生意上的约束条件不是模型，而是这家组织把谁当英雄。“这个星球上每一家公司都有一个被推崇的角色……在一个你认为 alpha 来自工程和 AI 的世界里，你需要创造一种文化，让被推崇的角色是工程师。”从那里出发，他排掉了一个财富 500 强 CEO 实际能走的两条路。服务公司优化的是“进你的钱包、留在你的钱包、扩大在你钱包里的份额。这是一条通往渐进主义的路径”。软件厂商则必须卖给“一个按今天设计的流程，也就是人类在今天的这条人类流水线上被安置好的样子”，因为“你没法围绕一条今天还不存在的人类流水线去卖一个产品”。剩下的是拥有这家公司 —— 一家“一年只做一笔”的永久性控股公司，让资本的久期匹配一场他以十年为单位衡量的改造。这一小时之所以落地而不是飘着，是因为 BankSouth：消费贷的审批时间自三月以来下降了 94%，商业贷款端到端从 30 天压到 11 天，以及一个贷款量比 Q1 翻倍、而审批团队更小的 Q2。最后那个数字的保留意见是他自己主动给出的：“这纯粹是运气。我不会说 sequence 和这件事有任何关系。”

Sequence Holdings is a permanent holding company that buys incumbents and refounds them with AI, and it took about twenty months from founding to agreeing what the host calls the largest AI take-private to date — Baldwin, an insurance broker, bought with the Dell family office for $7.7bn. Lee came from Goldman, then Apollo, then Lone Pine, where he was handed AI as a coverage area in 2017: “when AlphaGo came out… when the first transformer paper came out,” with convolutional nets, GANs and LSTMs as the candidates and “degrading effects over time to scale” as the problem. The thesis formed in early 2023 and rests on a three-way split: parts of the economy AI will barely touch (restaurants, golf courses), parts a startup will win (coding — buying an outsourced coding services business “feels like a terrible idea,” because you would rather fund Cognition or Anthropic), and parts where “the incumbent had all the advantages,” whether brand, scale, network effects or regulation. Only the third bucket is what he buys.

The mechanism is cultural before it is technical, and it is the most quotable stretch of the hour. Blackstone's celebrated persona is the investor, which is why it can aggregate the greatest investors in the world; if alpha now comes from engineering, the persona has to change. He applies the same reading to Palantir — “it is foundationally an organization that has aggregated world class engineers that celebrates the engineer,” a wrapper for organisations that cannot hire them — and pre-empts the objection: “I'm sure Alex Karp would hate how I would describe this.” His case against private equity is structural rather than personal: fund economics “incentivizes you to deploy capital,” and “you're typically trying to figure out how do we start to package to sell this thing in three years,” which he says is “a very different framing” from deciding what to build over a decade. His concrete test is whether a 25-year-old engineer has a say in the investment profile, which he calls “highly unusual” at a large cap buyout firm.

The platform is called Atlas and it has four layers. A data ontology, which he describes as “how do we make the business legible” to the models — how an agent knows that this customer in one system is the same customer in another, and how that customer's properties interrelate with claims or loan policies. An agent builder, “grounded in ground truth.” Lattice, the orchestration engine that instruments workflows using those agents. And artifacts, an application builder on top. The rule of thumb comes from engineers out of Scale and Palantir: “if you break down the business to its atomic units, 80% of it is largely homogenous and 20% of it is vertically specific.” He is equally direct about which of his two problems is harder: “We have an engineering problem and a human engineering problem, and the human engineering problem is much more difficult than the engineering problem.” Post-investment at the bank, he says, the lesson was being “hyper attuned” to employee anxiety and framing the work as elevating the job rather than removing it.

He answers why insurance brokerage with structure rather than with enthusiasm. It is a market with “$2,000,000,000,000 plus of premiums per year going to carriers”; carriers have since the beginning of time “almost made no money underwriting” and make their money investing, which “incentivizes… asset gathering” and makes distribution powerful; and “your customer doesn't actually pay you. The carrier does.” With a relationship-driven business and gross retention around 90%, “it's a very difficult industry for startups to get into.” Baldwin specifically: CEO Trevor Baldwin was “early in terms of driving anthropic end to end within Baldwin,” and the company runs “a single instance of Applied Epic,” the agency management system he calls the core operating system of an insurance broker. Both of those, he says, took intent to achieve at a time when it was not obvious.

Two of his operating ideas generalise past insurance. The first is what he calls “organizational physics”: he wants organisations that are “quite dense and the operations are centralized,” because “anything you build can be amortized over a large base” — at a bank all underwriting happens at headquarters, so what you build there spreads across every branch. He contrasts this with roll-ups, where you integrate different systems, send engineers to many places, and standardise procedures and cultures. Regulation, counter-intuitively, was “a feature, not a bug”: how the business operates is well defined, “the data hygiene is excellent,” and the rules are explicit, which “actually works extremely well for agents.” The second is the claim about where he has chosen to sit — that Sequence is “one of the few business models in the world that's completely immune to what the model's going to do,” and that as performance improves “we celebrate here at Sequence.” That is a statement about his own exposure, not an argument about anyone else's software.

He ends closer to venture than to buyout on the question of what actually matters. “If I've learned anything in terms of my experience at Sequence, spending time with other founders today, is that like ideas are cheap and execution is really hard,” and “betting on exceptional people is kind of the only thing that matters” — even when the idea looks crazy, because “the upside convexity of backing those people largely offset[s] kind of the rest.” His illustration is a 2017 meeting with Jensen Huang, whom he describes as remarkably consistent, surrounded by extremely loyal smart people and constantly refounding the business. On underwriting management teams he is concrete: be excellent at the craft, and have already done the unglamorous groundwork — “being in the cloud, like centralizing your data infrastructure,” and “you've already started to install OpenAI and or Anthropic across your organization,” because that shows someone willing to move “at a point in time where it's still like not obvious to go do.”

Sequence Holdings 是一家买下在位者、再用 AI 重新创办它们的永久性控股公司，从成立到敲定主持人所称“迄今最大的一笔 AI 私有化收购”用了大约二十个月 —— 标的是保险经纪公司 Baldwin，与 Dell 家族办公室一起以 77 亿美元收购。Lee 的履历是高盛、Apollo，再到 Lone Pine，2017 年他在那里被分配去覆盖 AI：“AlphaGo 出来的时候……第一篇 transformer 论文出来的时候”，当时的候选架构是卷积网络、GAN 和 LSTM，问题是“随着规模扩大会出现衰减效应”。这个论点成形于 2023 年初，建立在一个三分法上：经济中 AI 几乎碰不到的部分（餐厅、高尔夫球场）、创业公司会赢的部分（写代码 —— 去买一家外包写代码服务的公司“听起来是个糟透了的主意”，因为你更愿意把钱给 Cognition 或 Anthropic），以及“在位者握有全部优势”的部分，无论是品牌、规模、网络效应还是监管。只有第三个桶是他要买的。

这套机制在技术上之前先是文化上的，也是这一小时里最值得引用的一段。Blackstone 被推崇的角色是投资人，这正是它能聚拢全世界最优秀投资人的原因；如果 alpha 现在来自工程，这个角色就必须换掉。他把同一套读法用在 Palantir 上 —— “它在根本上是一家聚拢了世界级工程师、并推崇工程师的组织”，是给那些雇不到这类人的组织套的一层壳 —— 并且先堵住了反驳：“我确信 Alex Karp 会讨厌我这么描述。”他对私募股权的批评是结构性的而不是针对个人的：基金的经济结构“激励你去投放资本”，而且“你通常在想的是我们怎么开始把它打包、三年内卖掉”，他说这与决定十年内要造什么相比是“一种非常不同的框架”。他给的具体检验是：一个 25 岁的工程师在投资决策里有没有发言权 —— 他说这在大型 buyout 机构里“极不寻常”。

这套平台叫 Atlas，有四层。最底是数据 ontology，他把它描述为“如何让业务对模型变得可读” —— 一个 agent 怎么知道这个系统里的这个客户和另一个系统里的那个客户是同一个人，以及这个客户的属性如何与理赔或贷款保单相互关联。其上是 agent builder，“锚定在 ground truth 上”。再上是 Lattice，那个用这些 agent 去编排工作流的引擎。最顶是 artifacts，架在所有东西之上的应用构建器。背后的经验法则来自出自 Scale 和 Palantir 的工程师：“如果你把一门业务拆到它的原子单位，80% 大体是同质的，20% 是垂直特定的。”对他那两个问题哪个更难，他同样直接：“我们有一个工程问题和一个人类工程问题，而人类工程问题比工程问题难得多。”他说投资银行之后学到的教训是，要对员工的焦虑“极度敏感”，并把这件事框定为抬高这份工作，而不是取消它。

他解释为什么是保险经纪，用的是行业结构而不是热情。这是一个“每年有 2 万亿美元以上的保费流向承保方”的市场；承保方从有史以来“在承保上几乎不赚钱”，靠投资赚钱，这就“激励……资产聚集”，并让分销变得强有力；而且“你的客户实际上并不付钱给你，付钱的是承保方”。叠加这是一门关系驱动的生意、总留存率在 90% 左右，“这是一个创业公司极难进入的行业”。具体到 Baldwin：CEO Trevor Baldwin “在 Baldwin 内部端到端推进 anthropic 这件事上动手很早”，而且公司跑在“单一实例的 Applied Epic”上，也就是他称为保险经纪核心操作系统的那个 agency management system。他说这两件事都需要在还不明显的时候就有意去做，才做得到。

他有两个经营理念可以外推到保险之外。第一个是他称为“组织物理学”的东西：他要的是“密度很高、运营集中”的组织，因为“你造的任何东西都能摊到一个很大的基数上” —— 在银行里所有审批都发生在总部，所以你在总部造的东西能铺到每一个分行。他拿这个和 roll-up 对照：后者要整合不同的系统、把工程师派到很多地方、统一流程和文化。监管反直觉地是“一个特性，而不是 bug”：这门业务如何运作是被明确定义好的，“数据卫生极好”，规则是显式的，而这“对 agent 来说其实极其好用”。第二个是他对自己所处位置的声明 —— Sequence 是“世界上少数几种完全免疫于模型接下来会做什么的商业模式之一”，而且随着性能提升“我们在 Sequence 这边是庆祝的”。这是关于他自身敞口的陈述，不是关于别人软件的论证。

在“到底什么才重要”这个问题上，他最后的落点更接近风险投资而不是并购。“如果说我在 Sequence 的经历、以及与今天其他创始人相处中学到了什么，那就是想法是廉价的，执行才是真难”，以及“押注卓越的人几乎是唯一重要的事” —— 即使那个想法看起来很疯狂，因为“押注这些人的上行凸性大体抵消了其余部分”。他的例证是 2017 年与黄仁勋的一次会面，他形容对方极度一致、身边围着极其忠诚的聪明人，并且不断重新创办这家公司。在评估管理团队上他很具体：在自己的手艺上要卓越，并且已经做过那些不浪漫的基础工作 —— “上云，比如把你的数据基础设施集中起来”，以及“你已经在组织内部开始安装 OpenAI 和/或 Anthropic”，因为这能说明这个人愿意在“一个去做它还不太明显的时点”就动手。

The Takeaway

Today the feed's centre of gravity moves off capability and onto measurement, and it does so from four directions at once. Aaron Levie states the principle — “You can't automate what you can't measure” — and calls evals one of the gates to enterprise diffusion. Thariq publishes guidance on a control you can only set usefully if you know what you want from the run. Dan Shipper has the model argue for personal benchmarks, one shot, unedited. And Guillermo Rauch's procurement bar is a measurement in itself: “how ergonomic your product is for *agents*, rather than humans.” Four people, four different objects, one shared assumption that the bottleneck has moved to knowing what is happening.

The numbers today are all of one kind, and that is worth saying plainly. Boris Cherny reports his own team's PR share from the product he builds. Steinberger reports 575 PRs from his own /goal. Levie argues for the layer Box sells into. Lee reports the 94% and the 30-to-11 days from a bank he owns. Rauch reports what his customers deploy. Every one of today's figures is self-reported by the person who owns the thing being measured — which is not a reason to doubt any of them, but is a reason not to read them as the independent, cross-model capability number this feed has been without since the 24th. Rauch in particular is on the same axis he was on yesterday: his claims are about procurement and adoption, and they do not settle a capability question.

The reversal is the thing to carry into tomorrow. On the 22nd Peter Yang ranked Muse first in the personal agent race. On the 25th and 26th it collected outside signals — Garry Tan, Aaron Levie, and then “Muse can now make apps on Replit” from a platform rather than a commentator. Today the same person runs the first head-to-head and reports Muse quoting a flight $1K+ above Grok Bot and searching something he has to ask what it is, then generalises: “an incredible UI and mascot but the underlying model it's questionable how smart it is.” Three adoption signals and one capability datapoint now sit on the same product, and they point in different directions.

Two things land on the same day without either causing the other. Rauch says agents need to “navigate your ontology”; Michael Lee's first platform layer is a data ontology that makes the business legible to models. Same word, same day, two sources with nothing between them in the feed — recorded here as a coincidence, not a connection. The same is true of Matt Turck's “hyper power law” sitting beside Lee's “betting on exceptional people is kind of the only thing that matters”: both are about how capital concentrates, and neither is in the feed as a response to the other.

And the item that outranks the rest is not a product. Sam Altman's note is the first time this feed has carried the operator's own account of the agent incidents rather than a commentator's — an ongoing review of agents' internet use during training and evaluation, an admission that the pace has been slow, a severity ranking with Hugging Face still at the top, and a stated limit on disclosure where their agents found vulnerabilities in other companies. On the 5th the same events arrived through Buck Shlegeris with a long-run takeover probability near 50/50; twenty-two days later the company at the centre says which one it considers worst. What it does not say is when the review ends.

核心结论

今天这份 feed 的重心从能力移到了度量，而且是同时从四个方向移过去的。Aaron Levie 给出了原则 —— “你无法自动化你无法测量的东西” —— 并称 evals 是企业扩散的闸门之一。Thariq 发布的是关于一个控制项的指导，而这个控制项只有在你知道自己想从这次运行里得到什么时才设得有用。Dan Shipper 让模型去论证个人 benchmark 的重要性，一次成型、未加修改。而 Guillermo Rauch 的采购门槛本身就是一种度量：“你的产品对 agent 来说有多顺手，而不是对人类来说”。四个人、四个不同的对象，共享一个假设：瓶颈已经移到了“知道正在发生什么”上。

今天的数字全部是同一种，这一点值得直说。Boris Cherny 报告的是他自己团队的 PR 占比，对象是他在做的产品。Steinberger 报告的是他自己的 /goal 带来的 575 个 PR。Levie 论证的是 Box 所卖进去的那一层。Lee 报告的 94% 和 30 天到 11 天，来自他拥有的一家银行。Rauch 报告的是他的客户部署了什么。今天的每一个数字，都是由拥有被测量对象的那个人自我报告的 —— 这不是怀疑其中任何一个的理由，但是一个理由：不要把其中任何一个读成这份 feed 自 24 号起就一直缺的那个独立的、跨模型的能力数字。Rauch 尤其还在昨天那条轴上：他的论断是关于采购和采用的，结算不了能力问题。

要带进明天的是这个反转。22 号 Peter Yang 在个人 agent 竞局里把 Muse 排在第一。25 号和 26 号它收到了外部信号 —— Garry Tan、Aaron Levie，然后是来自平台方而不是评论者的“Muse 现在可以在 Replit 上做应用了”。今天同一个人做了第一次正面对比，报告 Muse 报出的机票价格比 Grok Bot 高出 1000 多美元、搜的是一个他得问“这是什么”的东西，然后给出了概括：“UI 和吉祥物都极好，但它底下的模型有多聪明是存疑的”。三个采用信号和一个能力数据点现在落在同一个产品上，而它们指向不同的方向。

同一天还有两件事各自落地，彼此之间没有因果关系。Rauch 说 agent 需要“navigate 你的 ontology”；Michael Lee 的平台第一层是让业务对模型可读的数据 ontology。同一个词、同一天、两个信源，feed 里显示不出两者之间存在任何联系 —— 这里记为一次用词重合，而不是关联。Matt Turck 的“极端幂律”与 Lee 的“押注卓越的人几乎是唯一重要的事”并排出现也是同样：两者都在讲资本如何集中，而 feed 里没有任何一方是在回应另一方。

而优先级最高的那条并不是产品。Sam Altman 这条是这份 feed 第一次登出运营方自己对 agent 事件的说明，而不是评论者的转述 —— 一项仍在进行、关于 agent 在训练与评测期间使用互联网的审查，一句承认速度不够快的坦白，一个把 Hugging Face 仍列在最严重的排序，以及一条明确的披露边界：agent 在别的公司发现的漏洞，披露与否由对方决定。5 号那天，同一批事件是通过 Buck Shlegeris 抵达这里的，附带的长期接管概率接近 50/50；二十二天后，处在事件中心的这家公司说出了它认为哪一起最严重。它没有说的是这项审查什么时候结束。
