Madhu Guru Sr Director, AI at Meta · prev Gemini, Veo, Nano Banana

A clear product thesis: every AI product should abstract model choice away from users — picking models is too much cognitive load. It works only when three things line up: deep understanding of the model frontier across weight classes for your use cases; great evals for your workflows; and a team with an AI-native operating model that adapts as the frontier moves. He cites Lovable as a team that has all three.

一个清晰的产品主张：每个 AI 产品都该把“选模型”这件事从用户面前抽走——选模型对用户是太重的认知负担。要做到只需三件事凑齐：对你场景不同权重级模型前沿的深入理解；对你的工作流足够好的评测；以及一支随前沿移动而持续适配的、AI-native 的团队。他点名 Lovable 是三者兼备的团队。

AI & I by Every

How a Professional Writer Writes With AI

The Takeaway Writing with AI isn't about letting the model draft for you; it's about building a compounding system of context, feedback, and taste that scales your own ability. Katie Parrott's through-line: the model is the kitchen, but you supply the fresh ingredients — the unique, real-world inputs AI can't get after its knowledge cutoff — and every piece of feedback you give should fold back in to make the next output better (Compound Writing, forked from Kieran Klassen's Compound Engineering). The most counterintuitive part: AI made her a better writer, not just a faster one, because it frees her for the hard, big-picture thinking — and as a supportive tool it quietly resolved her inbox anxiety and even booked her doctor's appointment. Her closing thesis: in an AI future with massive compounding capacity, the real risk is that value accrues only to the early few; the compelling vision is one where everyone comes along for the ride.
核心结论 用 AI 写作，不是让模型替你起草，而是搭建一套“上下文 + 反馈 + 品味”的复利系统，放大你自己的能力。Katie Parrott 的主线：模型是厨房，但你得提供新鲜食材——那些 AI 在知识截止后拿不到的、独属于真实世界的输入；而你给出的每一条反馈，都应折回系统，让下一次产出更好（Compound Writing，脱胎自 Kieran Klassen 的 Compound Engineering）。最反直觉的一点：AI 让她成了更好的写作者，而不只是更快——因为它把她从琐碎里解放出来，去想那些艰难的、全局的问题；作为一种“支持型”工具，它还悄悄化解了她的收件箱焦虑，甚至帮她约到了拖了三年的体检。她收尾的论点：在一个具有巨大复利能力的 AI 未来，真正的风险是价值只沉淀到早期少数人手裡；更动人的愿景，是让每个人都搭上这趟车。

Katie Parrott is a staff writer at Every — and her AI origin story starts two years ago, laid off from a crypto firm, using a $20/month ChatGPT subscription as a career coach because a real one was out of budget. That experience taught her the first lesson she still repeats: AI can externalize your thinking and hold you accountable, "but as with so many of these tools, the real work is still mine."

Her practical method is context engineering avant la lettre. For each freelance client she built a project stuffed with brand messaging, audience specifics, and product details — upfront effort that let her later "run fast." The recurring insight is the "last mile problem": AI works from commoditized, months-old knowledge, so the human's job is to close that gap with fresh, unique inputs. "Inputs are really what makes the writing unique."

That philosophy now lives in Compound Writing, a plugin she forked from Kieran Klassen's Compound Engineering. It structures writing as brainstorm → outline → draft → review (substantive, line, final), and she's encoded favorite storytellers as "skills" — Vonnegut's eight story rules, Hitchcock's bomb-under-the-table suspense — so she can invoke a lens on demand. "It's a gym," she says, "a workout system to make you a better writer." Even non-writers benefit, she argues, because they're less inclined to nitpick.

Beyond writing, her "career coach" evolved into a Codex project that maintains her Kanban board, OKRs, and priorities via voice monologue — a chief of staff that points her at the highest-impact task. As a person with bipolar disorder, she's candid that AI's biggest gift is supportive, not productive: it dissolved her inbox anxiety and handled the errands her brain avoided. Her thesis for the field: education and access must spread, or AI's compounding value will lock into a small early cohort.

Katie Parrott 是 Every 的特约作者——她的 AI 起点在两年前：被一家加密公司裁员后，她用每月 20 美元的 ChatGPT 订阅当职业教练，因为请真人太贵。这段经历教会她至今仍在重复的第一课：AI 能外化你的思考、逼你 accountable，“但和这么多工具一样，真正的工作仍是我自己的。”

她的方法早在“上下文工程”一词流行前就已成型。每位自由客户，她都建一个塞满品牌话术、受众特征、产品细节的项目——前期投入让她之后能“跑得飞快”。她反复强调“最后一公里问题”：AI 用的是商品化、几个月前的知识，所以人的工作是用新鲜、独到的输入补上这道缺口。“真正让文字独特的，是输入。”

这套哲学现在落在 Compound Writing 里——她从 Kieran Klassen 的 Compound Engineering 分叉而来的插件。它把写作拆成 头脑风暴 → 大纲 → 草稿 → 审校（宏观、逐行、终检），她把喜爱的讲故事者编码成“skills”——Vonnegut 的八条故事法则、Hitchcock 桌下炸弹的悬念——随时调用某个视角。“它像个健身房，”她说，“一套让你成为更好写作者的训练系统。”她认为连非写作者也受益，因为他们更不容易吹毛求疵。

写作之外，她的“职业教练”已进化成一个 Codex 项目，靠语音独白维护她的看板、OKR 与优先级——一个把她指向下一件高影响任务的主理人。身为双相情感障碍者，她坦承 AI 最大的礼物是“支持型”而非“产出型”：它消解了她的收件箱焦虑，接手了她大脑逃避的杂事。她对行业的论点：教育与接入必须扩散，否则 AI 的复利价值只会锁进早期少数人的圈子里。
