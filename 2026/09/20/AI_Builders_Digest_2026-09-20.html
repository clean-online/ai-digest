Why Diffusion Will Win AI Inference with Inception Co-Founder and CEO Stefano Ermon
The Takeaway Ermon's argument is architectural, and it turns on where the parallelism lives. Autoregressive models parallelised training in 2017 when RNNs gave way to transformers, but inference is still one token at a time — "extremely memory bound," moving weights around the memory hierarchy while doing "very little arithmetic." A diffusion model's inference workload "is basically very, very similar to the workload you have for training," so it is the thing GPUs are actually built for. His closing rule is the bitter lesson applied one level down: "the more parallel solution is the one that is eventually going to win," because economics are "dominated by the kind of intelligence per watt, the intelligence per dollar."
核心结论 Ermon 的论点是架构层面的，而它落在"并行性在哪里"这一点上。自回归模型在 2017 年把训练并行化了（RNN 让位给 transformer），但推理仍然是一次一个 token——"极度受内存带宽限制"，大部分时间在内存层级之间搬权重，而"做的算术极少"。扩散模型的推理负载"和你训练时的负载非常非常相似"，因此它正是 GPU 真正擅长的那种工作负载。他的收尾法则是把 bitter lesson 往下一层套用："越并行的方案最终越会赢"，因为经济性"取决于你能从模型里拿到多少每瓦特智能、每美元智能"。

Ermon is a Stanford professor and now co-founder and CEO of Inception, and the company is small and live: "about two years old, around 50 people," with Mercury models "on par with the Haiku models, flash models, mini nano models from OpenAI, if you look at benchmarks, while being significantly faster," and "we are serving these models in production today." The result that started it is the 2024 paper — at GPT-2 scale, under a billion parameters, a transformer trained as a diffusion model matched an autoregressive model's perplexity on the same data, "and the diffusion model was significantly faster... we were able to generate text like 10x faster."

The hardware argument is the part that generalises past his own company. Autoregressive decoding "does not map well to GPUs" because it is sequential and memory bound; diffusion processes many tokens at once, so its inference looks like training. He extends it to post-training: with reasoning models "a lot of it is scaling test and compute," and with RL "a lot of the bottleneck is generating rollouts," so a model that scales better at inference also scales better during RL. On hardware, the gains are "multiplicative" rather than competitive — one named customer, OpenCall, was serving its voice-agent LLM on Cerebras custom chips and switched to Inception's diffusion models to get "the same speed... on NVIDIA GPUs, which means much more availability... and lower costs, higher quality," with an OpenAI-compatible API so the surrounding harness did not change.

Two claims are worth separating from the pitch. On controllability: an autoregressive model makes you "wait until you've generated the full object" before you can score it against a constraint, whereas diffusion is coarse-to-fine and can be steered mid-generation by an external reward function — "there is a lot of evidence in the academic literature, at least, that diffusion models are easier to control." On data: denoising is effectively data augmentation, so diffusion models "tend to be a little bit more data efficient," which only becomes decisive "where data becomes the bottleneck." He sizes the near-term market himself rather than claiming the whole thing — from OpenRouter's task taxonomy he estimates that "between 20-30%" of workloads are ones "where latency is really, really important."

The costs he names are ecosystem costs, and they are honest. Diffusion LLMs "cannot run on vLLM or SGLang," so Inception "had to build our own serving engine"; the SFT and RLHF stack is also built in-house; and because they chose not to open source, "there is less an opportunity for the community to contribute... it's harder to adopt. It's hard to do on prem kind of like deployments." His moat is described the same way — IP and trade secrets first, then the serving engine ("if you don't have the serving engine... you're still stuck"), then evals and data built from real customers. Asked about recursive self-improvement, he is explicit that it is not here yet: models have "accelerated our development process a lot," but "right now the human ingenuity is still super important." The academic record he closes on is his own group's: diffusion with Yang Song in 2019, FlashAttention, and DPO.

Ermon 是斯坦福教授，现在是 Inception 的联合创始人兼 CEO，而这家公司规模不大、但已经在跑："成立约两年，约 50 人"，Mercury 模型"如果看基准测试，与 OpenAI 的 Haiku、flash、mini nano 这类模型质量相当，但明显更快"，而且"我们今天已经在生产环境服务这些模型"。一切的起点是 2024 年那篇论文——在 GPT-2 规模、不到十亿参数的条件下，一个按扩散方式训练的 transformer 在同样数据上追平了自回归模型的困惑度，"而扩散模型明显更快……我们生成文本的速度能快大约 10 倍"。

硬件那层论证是可以外推到他自己公司之外的部分。自回归解码"不能很好地映射到 GPU 上"，因为它是串行且受内存带宽限制的；扩散模型一次处理许多 token，所以它的推理形态和训练相似。他把这个论证延伸到了后训练：推理模型"很大一部分是在扩展测试时计算"，而 RL"很大一部分瓶颈在于生成 rollout"，因此一个在推理侧扩展性更好的模型，在 RL 阶段也会扩展得更好。关于硬件，两方面的收益是"相乘"的而不是互相竞争的——他点名的一个客户 OpenCall，原本把语音 agent 的 LLM 跑在 Cerebras 的定制芯片上，后来切换到 Inception 的扩散模型，为的是"在 NVIDIA GPU 上拿到同样的速度，这意味着可得性高得多……而且成本更低、质量更高"，同时 API 与 OpenAI 兼容，因此外层的 harness 不用改。

有两个主张值得从推销中摘出来单独看。关于可控性：自回归模型必须"等整个对象生成完"才能拿它对某个约束打分，而扩散是由粗到细生成的，可以在生成中途被外部奖励函数引导——"至少在学术文献里有大量证据表明，扩散模型更容易控制"。关于数据：去噪本身等价于数据增强，所以扩散模型"往往数据效率略高一些"，而这只有在"数据成为瓶颈的地方"才变得决定性。他给出近期市场规模时是自己在估、而不是宣称拿下全部——参照 OpenRouter 的任务分类，他估计"大约 20%–30%"的工作负载属于"延迟极其重要"的那一类。

他列出的成本都是生态层面的成本，而且说得很诚实。扩散 LLM"没法跑在 vLLM 或 SGLang 上"，所以 Inception"不得不自建服务引擎"；SFT 与 RLHF 的整套工具链也是自研的；而因为选择了不开源，"社区参与贡献的机会就更少……采用难度更高。做本地化部署也很难。"他描述护城河的方式是一样的——先是 IP 与商业秘密，然后是那个服务引擎（"如果你没有服务引擎……你还是卡住"），再是基于真实客户建起来的评测与数据。被问到递归自我改进时，他说得很明白：这还没到——模型"大幅加速了我们的开发流程"，但"眼下人的创造力仍然极其重要"。最后他落在自己课题组的学术记录上：2019 年与杨Song一起做出的扩散工作、FlashAttention，以及 DPO。

The Takeaway

Today the same thesis is argued from two ends, and the two ends are about four hours apart. On the product end it is Jev: Nikunj Kothari's "3000 kid snacks with multiple criteria in 28 seconds for $0.11," and Aaron Levie's Box demo where an incident report is classified, routed into escalate/monitor/review, and tagged — "This all happens nearly instantly and at almost no cost." On the architecture end it is Stefano Ermon on No Priors, arguing that autoregressive inference is "extremely memory bound" while diffusion inference "is basically very, very similar to the workload you have for training," so "the more parallel solution is the one that is eventually going to win." Both are saying that the binding constraint has moved from capability to cost-and-latency, and Rauch names the mood behind it: "downstream of the 'AI is too expensive/slow' zeitgeist."

Jev is worth reading as a sequence rather than a novelty. It was in this digest on the 18th as an announcement — Rauch's "up to 18x faster (p95) and more accurate," heading into Vercel AI Gateway and "likely new default," with Dan Shipper's red-flag version of the same name, "jev is just a JSON classifier." Two days later the name carries usage: "The data and the anecdata on Jev's adoption are shocking," free on Vercel, a community directory built for it, and a Box demo attached. Levie's list is where the volume actually comes from — "insurance claims, contract management, loan processing, security reviews, customer log analysis" — which is the enterprise backlog, not the chat window.

Rauch's gateway numbers are the one figure to keep an eye on, with its own caveat attached. Open models were 78.4% of token volume on Vercel AI Gateway on what he thinks may be a record day, but spend runs differently: Moonshot AI and DeepSeek were #3 and #4, and "adding Z.ai, their combined spend surpasses OpenAI (#2)" — with his own note that this is "spend for inference of the model across providers (mostly in the US), not revenue going directly to the open weight labs." Volume share and money share are not the same number, and he said so before anyone else could.

The plumbing is moving in a quieter way. Thariq's AGENTS.md support — "if there is no CLAUDE.md in a folder, Claude will check for and use AGENTS.md," built on "Claude Code mods" — is a convention that started outside Anthropic being read natively, and implemented as a layer you can replace. Steinberger's roboclaw "runs our team server, is live on Discord... and knows about all the sessions it's juggling," which is the same shift from one machine to one shared context, done at team scale.

One consumer datapoint that is easy to file under anecdote and probably should not be: Peter Yang says Muse "save me $800+ a year on my cable and phone bills," and posts the transcript of it negotiating "$288 in annual savings" off a Comcast bill. His inference is the sentence that matters — "most companies' customer support lines are NOT ready for agents." That is a cost argument pointed at the other side of the phone.

核心结论

今天同一个论点从两端被论证，而两端相隔大约四小时。产品那一端是 Jev：Nikunj Kothari 的"按多个标准给 3,000 种儿童零食打分，28 秒、0.11 美元"，以及 Aaron Levie 的 Box 演示——一份事故报告被分类、路由进 escalate/monitor/review，并被打上标签，"这一切几乎瞬间完成，而且成本几乎为零"。架构那一端是 No Priors 上的 Stefano Ermon：自回归推理"极度受内存带宽限制"，而扩散推理"和训练时的负载非常非常相似"，所以"越并行的方案最终越会赢"。两边讲的是同一件事：约束条件已经从"能力"移到了"成本与延迟"。而 Rauch 给这股情绪命了名："'AI 太贵/太慢'这个时代情绪的下游产物"。

Jev 值得当作一条序列来读，而不是一个新奇玩意儿。18 号那天它在本刊里还是一条发布消息——Rauch 说它"最高快 18 倍（p95）而且更准"，将进入 Vercel AI Gateway 并"很可能成为新的默认"，同时 Dan Shipper 给同一个名字配了插红旗的版本："jev 不过是个 JSON 分类器"。两天之后，这个名字带上了使用量："关于 Jev 采用情况的数据和坊间反馈都惊人"，在 Vercel 上免费，有人为它建了社区目录，还挂上了一个 Box 演示。而真正撑起量的是 Levie 那串清单——"保险理赔、合同管理、贷款审批、安全审查、客户日志分析"——那是企业的积压活，不是聊天窗口。

Rauch 那组网关数字是最值得盯住的一个指标，但也自带免责说明。在他认为可能创纪录的一天里，开放模型占了 Vercel AI Gateway 上 78.4% 的 token 量，但支出的排序不同：Moonshot AI 和 DeepSeek 排在第 3、第 4，而"加上 Z.ai，它们的支出合计超过了 OpenAI（第 2）"——他自己注明这是"模型在各供应商上的推理支出，主要发生在美国，不是直接流向开放权重实验室的收入"。量份额和钱份额不是一个数字，而他先把这句话说了。

底层管道正在以更安静的方式迁移。Thariq 那条 AGENTS.md 支持——"如果某个文件夹里没有 CLAUDE.md，Claude 会去找并使用 AGENTS.md"，构建在 "Claude Code mods" 之上——是一个起源于 Anthropic 之外的约定被原生读取，而且是以一层可替换的机制实现的。Steinberger 的 roboclaw"跑着我们的团队服务器，挂在 Discord 上……并且知道它正在同时处理的所有会话"，这是同一个迁移（从一台机器到一个共享上下文）在团队尺度上的版本。

有一条消费侧的数据点，很容易被归为轶事、但大概不该：Peter Yang 说 Muse"在我的有线电视和电话账单上每年帮我省了 800 多美元"，并贴出了它从 Comcast 账单上谈下"每年 288 美元节省"的通话记录。他从中推出的那句才是关键——"大多数公司的客服热线还没准备好面对 agent"。这是一个把成本论证对准电话另一头的判断。
