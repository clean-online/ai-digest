Thibault Sottiaux Codex & ChatGPT, OpenAI

Sottiaux posts the week's shipping list on a single line — Images 2.5, GPT-Live-1, Agents API, Data Agent, ChatGPT for Financial Services — and notes that "it's not yet DevDay," with more planned for next week. The more useful post is his quality reset and postmortem. Three fixes: skills written for previous models were "triggering too often or preventing the model from checking its work"; an opt-in context management experiment that could cause early stops or replies to older messages has been disabled, with a rough estimate that 4–5k users were affected; and some badly configured engines causing measured quality degradation on long-tail traffic have been removed. A reset lands by midnight. His third post is an acquisition: Aidan and Sasha from the Git AI team join OpenAI, keeping the open-source tool that shows how coding agents contribute to a codebase.

Madhu Guru Sr Director, AI at Meta

Guru's thread is the most concrete diagnosis of the day on why enterprise AI efforts fail, and none of his three reasons is about model quality. First, old-school playbooks: a trusted lieutenant is picked to run a central AI team, but "the team structure, product patterns, how you launch and learn that got you here do not work with AI" — fifteen years of incremental improvement does not prepare a company for something that requires experimentation and invention. Second, under-investing in evals. Third, building from the outside: the central team declares itself a platform team and ships tools disconnected from the actual workflows, context and judgment of the people doing the work, producing "begrudging adoption" rather than productivity gains. The remedies are organisational — hire leaders who have actually built AI products, make evals first class, and embed your best AI builders inside the functions you want to transform: build *with* finance, sales, support. His second post is a bare link.

Guru 的这条 thread 是今天对企业 AI 失败原因最具体的诊断，而他给的三条理由没有一条关于模型质量。第一是沿用旧打法：让一位可信的副手去带一个中央 AI 团队，但"把你带到今天的团队结构、产品范式、发布与学习的方式，对 AI 都不适用"——十五年的渐进式改进，并不能让公司准备好面对一件需要实验和发明的事。第二是在 evals 上投入不足。第三是从外部为公司做 AI：中央团队自封为平台团队，做出来的工具与实际工作流、上下文和一线判断脱节，结果是"勉强的采纳"，而不是生产力提升。他给的药方都是组织层面的——招真正做过 AI 产品的负责人、把 evals 列为一等公民、把最强的 AI builder 嵌进你要改造的业务里：和财务一起做、和销售一起做、和支持一起做。第二条帖子只有一个裸链接。

Coinbase's Everything Exchange: Agentic Finance, Stablecoins, and Tokenization with CEO Brian Armstrong
The Takeaway Armstrong's thesis is that crypto was built for humans but will be required by agents. The number that carries the argument is a payments statistic: card rails carry a ~30¢ flat minimum, and "about 76% of the agent ecommerce transactions we're seeing are under 30¢" — so the machine economy needs a rail the human one never needed. Hence a self-custodial account an agent can open with a pasted prompt, no KYC, because "our AI agents don't have a government ID."
核心结论 Armstrong 的论点是：加密轨道是为人类建的，但会被 agent 所需要。撑起这个论点的是一个支付数字——银行卡通道有约 30 美分的固定最低费用，而"我们看到的 agent 电商交易中约有 76% 低于 30 美分"，所以机器经济需要一条人类经济从未需要的轨道。于是有了那个 agent 粘贴一段提示就能开立的自托管账户，无需 KYC，因为"我们的 AI agent 没有政府颁发的身份证"。

Armstrong opens on agentic commerce rather than on crypto. Debit and credit rails carry a flat ~30¢ minimum plus a percentage, which "doesn't make sense to pay 30¢ to send a 1¢ payment," and the transactions agents actually generate are mostly information gathering — compiling paywalled research, scraping data, or agents calling other specialized agents like a tool call. So Coinbase shipped a single-command prompt that gives an agent its own financial account on a self-custodial wallet: no KYC, because agents have no government ID and "can't walk into a bank branch, at least not yet without their humanoid robot companion." His framing: "we don't want the AIs to be unbanked."

The business is described in three layers. The Everything Exchange puts every asset class on chain with cross margin — stocks, commodities, crypto, perps, prediction markets. Stablecoin payments are "under one second, under 1¢" and keep growing even when Bitcoin is down. Agentic Finance gives every retail user the kind of advisor only wealthy people had — dollar cost averaging, tax loss harvesting, legging into a portfolio when the market dips. On the numbers he will cite: 88% of Coinbase revenue is now non-Bitcoin trading; the tokenized stock product is the first that is a real security held one-to-one in custody rather than a synthetic, currently ex-US and in discussion with the SEC; and prediction markets hit roughly a $100M revenue run rate within months of launch, growing over 100% quarter over quarter. He also notes about four billion people have no access to any brokerage account.

Two arguments are worth separating from the pitch. On specialists: frontier models are trained as generalists, but a small open-weight model fine-tuned on a company's own cases — a compliance review queue trained on 100,000 Coinbase cases — can outperform them on that task, so he expects a market of narrow agents. On the "there will be no money" argument, he disagrees in any relevant timeframe: even with heavy deflation, land, energy and chips stay scarce, so "we're still going to need a medium of exchange," and "crypto was really, really good for humans, and it's going to be essential for AI." Asked whether AI shrinks companies, he says tasks are eliminated, not people — the existing team ships more.

The back half turns to New Limit, the longevity company he co-founded. The approach is epigenetic reprogramming — restoring function cells had when younger without changing their type — and the search space of transcription factors is large enough that they "built the leading frontier model for epigenetic reprogramming" to recommend the next pooled screening experiments. First three cell types: hepatocytes, vasculature and T cells. Following the GLP-1 pattern, phase one targets alcoholic liver disease, where twelve-month survivability after diagnosis is very low and the only option is transplant; he estimates around $20B if that drug alone works, but the goal is a cocktail that lets a healthy 40-year-old have the liver, immune system or skin of a 20-year-old. The logic is root-cause: "most of the diseases that take us out are all highly correlated with age." He closes on special economic zones — Coinbase invested in Prospera in Honduras — arguing that over-regulation is what slows progress and that the US should carve out federal land where data centres, nuclear plants and drone delivery can be built without the normal EPA and FAA process, because "you can't really solve a problem as an entrepreneur unless you can iterate on it."

Armstrong 从 agent 电商开场，而不是从加密开场。借记卡和信用卡通道有约 30 美分的固定最低费加抽成，"为了转 1 美分付 30 美分说不通"，而 agent 实际产生的交易大多是信息收集——整理付费墙后的研究资料、抓取数据，或者 agent 像调用工具一样去调用其他专业 agent。于是 Coinbase 做了一个"一条命令"的提示，让 agent 在自托管钱包上拥有自己的金融账户：没有 KYC，因为 agent 没有政府身份证，"也没法走进银行网点，至少在有人形机器人陪着之前还不行"。他的说法是："我们不想让 AI 成为没有银行服务的人。"

业务被分成三层。Everything Exchange 把各类资产都放到链上并支持交叉保证金——股票、大宗、加密货币、永续合约、预测市场。稳定币支付"不到一秒、不到 1 美分"，而且在比特币下跌时仍在增长。Agentic Finance 则是让每个普通用户都拥有过去只有富人才有的顾问——定投、税损收割、市场回调时按比例分批建仓。他愿意引用的数字有：Coinbase 88% 的收入已经来自非比特币交易；代币化股票产品是第一个由托管中真实证券 1:1 支撑、而非合成品的产品，目前只在美国以外开放，正与 SEC 沟通；预测市场上线数月就达到约 1 亿美元年收入化规模，环比增速超过 100%。他还提到全球约有 40 亿人没有任何券商账户。

有两个论点值得从推销中摘出来单独看。关于专业 agent：前沿模型被训练成通才，但用公司自有案例微调过的小参数开源模型——比如用 Coinbase 十万条合规案例训练出来的合规审核队列模型——可以在该任务上超过前沿模型，所以他预期会出现一个窄领域 agent 的市场。关于"未来不会有货币"的说法，他在任何相关时间尺度上都不同意：即便出现大幅通缩，土地、能源和芯片依然稀缺，因此"我们仍然需要交换媒介"，而"加密对人类非常好，对 AI 则会是必需的"。被问到 AI 是否会让公司变小，他说被消灭的是任务而不是人——现有团队能产出更多。

后半段转向他联合创办的长寿公司 New Limit。路径是表观遗传重编程——在不改变细胞类型的前提下，恢复细胞年轻时的功能——而转录因子的搜索空间大到他们"为表观遗传重编程建了领先的前沿模型"，用来推荐下一轮大规模混合筛选实验。前三类细胞是：肝细胞、血管细胞和 T 细胞。参照 GLP-1 的路径，一期临床选择酒精性肝病，这类患者确诊后十二个月生存率很低、唯一选项是肝移植；他估计单是这个药若能成，价值约 200 亿美元，但目标是一种组合疗法，让 40 岁健康人的肝脏、免疫系统或皮肤回到 20 岁的状态。底层逻辑是针对根因："夺走我们生命的大多数疾病，都与年龄高度相关。"最后他谈到特殊经济区——Coinbase 投资了洪都拉斯的 Prospera——认为拖慢进步的是过度监管，美国应该划出联邦土地，让数据中心、核电站和无人机配送可以在不走常规 EPA、FAA 流程的情况下建成，因为"作为创业者，你没法在不能试错迭代的情况下真正解决问题"。
