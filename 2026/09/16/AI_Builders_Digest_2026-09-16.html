Aaron Levie CEO, Box

Box CEO Aaron Levie writes the day's most concrete forecast, and the interesting part is the number he attaches to how early it still is. The stack he lists — "agent swarms, better computer use, the next wave of APIs and MCPs coming online, and new form factors like Muse or Instinct, vertical enterprise agents, background workflow agents" — leads to one claim: "We're going to throw agents at vastly more tasks in our professional and personal lives than we had initially imagined." His multiplier is about volume rather than capability: what agents go out and find for us "is going to be 100X more volume than what we can imagine previously prompting in a single session." His examples are deliberately unglamorous — agents that "surgically recruit talent for you 24/7," watch "every signal in your customer's business for when to pitch them," and "review every line of code written for security issues and bugs." Then the calibration that keeps it from being hype: "We're probably 1% of the way into the shape of what all these agents look like, where they get deployed from, how they get managed, how we budget for them, and so on."

Box CEO Aaron Levie 给出了今天最具体的一份预测，而真正有意思的是他给"现在有多早"标上的数字。他列出的那一摞东西——"agent 集群、更好的 computer use、即将上线的新一波 API 与 MCP，以及 Muse 或 Instinct 这类新形态、垂直领域企业 agent、后台工作流 agent"——导向一个判断："我们在职业和个人生活里，会把 agent 扔向远比最初设想多得多的任务。"他说的倍数是关于体量而不是能力：agent 替我们出去找回来、在后台做完的事，"体量会比我们此前想象的单次会话提示多出 100 倍"。他举的例子刻意不酷炫——"24 小时不间断替你精准招人"、盯着"客户公司里的每一个信号判断什么时候该去推销"、以及"逐行审查写下的每一行代码有没有安全问题和 bug"。而让这段话不至于变成吹嘘的是他那句校准："我们大概只走完了 1%——关于这些 agent 长什么样、从哪里被部署、怎么被管理、怎么给它们做预算，等等。"

His second post is the bill for the first one, and it is the clearest statement of the trade-off anyone has offered this week: "Protecting enterprise data in a world where agents are using our systems 100X more than people ever did is going to be one of the more complex security and governance challenges of the 21st century." The bind is symmetric — "If you give an agent too much unfettered information access, it will be difficult to truly control and protect your data; and conversely, if you lock everything down completely, you won't get any real productivity gains from AI." Levie puts his own product on the table rather than only naming the problem: Box Shield now offers "granular controls on what content agents can and can't work with based on document classification level," and is building features that "automatically detect and alert (or block) when data is being accessed or used in unusual or anomalous ways by agents." He frames the rest as an industry-wide build-out, naming OpenAI and Anthropic alongside Palo Alto Networks, Cisco, CrowdStrike and Okta, plus startups Enclave, Method, Alterion and Runlayer.

他的第二条是第一条的账单，也是本周所有人对这个取舍最清楚的表述："在一个 agent 使用我们系统的频次比人类高出 100 倍的世界里保护企业数据，会是 21 世纪更复杂的几个安全与治理挑战之一。"这个两难是对称的——"如果你给 agent 太多不受约束的信息访问权限，你的数据就很难真正被控制和保护；反过来，如果你把一切全锁死，你又拿不到任何真实的 AI 生产力收益。"Levie 把自己家的产品摆上了桌，而不只是点出问题：Box Shield 现在提供"基于文档密级的细粒度控制，决定 agent 能动哪些内容、不能动哪些"，并且正在做"当数据被 agent 以异常或反常方式访问使用时，自动检测并告警（或阻断）"的能力。他把剩下的部分描述成一场全行业的建设，点名了 OpenAI 与 Anthropic，也点名了 Palo Alto Networks、Cisco、CrowdStrike、Okta，以及 Enclave、Method、Alterion、Runlayer 这些创业公司。

Guillermo Rauch CEO, Vercel

Vercel CEO Guillermo Rauch posts the day's cleanest engineering thesis in one line: "Agents are only as good as the proof-checkers, compilers, type systems, and linters you give them." The product he attaches to it is shadcn/lint, which "helps agents stay on track with the rules of your design system," and the generalization is the part worth keeping: "Meta: verifiers + skills are the new 'frameworks'!" His second post is a hire that reads as a bet on where the layer below agents is going. Steren, "creator of Google Cloud Run," joins Vercel to lead the Fluid family of compute products (Functions, Containers, Sandbox, Builds), and Rauch's framing is historical: "Serverless was the last chapter of the cloud, and Steren helped define the paradigm at Google. Agents are the next frontier, and they require new compute primitives designed for them." The third is a product note on fx — it "auto-upgrades," ctrl+g "restarts + resumes the chat," and 0.0.10 is "much, much faster for longer sessions."

Vercel CEO Guillermo Rauch 用一句话写下了今天最干净的工程判断："agent 的上限，取决于你给它配的证明检查器、编译器、类型系统和 linter。"他挂在后面的产品是 shadcn/lint，用来"帮 agent 遵守你设计系统的规则"，而真正值得记住的是他那句推广："verifiers + skills 就是新的‘框架’！"他的第二条是一则招聘，读起来像是在赌 agent 下面那一层会往哪走。Google Cloud Run 的创造者 Steren 加入 Vercel，领导 Fluid 系列计算产品（Functions、Containers、Sandbox、Builds），Rauch 的说法带着历史感："Serverless 是云的上一章，而 Steren 在 Google 参与定义了那个范式。Agent 是下一个前沿，而它们需要为它们重新设计的计算原语。"第三条是 fx 的产品说明——它"会自动升级"，ctrl+g 可以"重启并恢复会话"，0.0.10 在长会话下"快非常多"。

Nikunj Kothari Partner, FPV Ventures

FPV Ventures partner Nikunj Kothari's substantive post is an investment memo, and the argument is a structural one about payments rather than an AI story. Piston removes the fuel card entirely: "It moves the payment over its own rails without networks or any intermediaries. Piston pays the station and then invoices the fleet." The reason is that fraud and reconciliation are properties of the card, not bugs in it — "a lot of these cards are not even tied to a particular driver. They often get stolen, skimmed, or misused. The fleet owner then has to eat the fraud and then spend a long time reconciling what exactly happened." Every transaction is "tied to a specific driver with the time, location, and fuel type attached." His numbers: payment volume "grown 8x," merchant network "grown 40x," retention "above 98%," live "across 2,000 stations and across 48 states," and fleets "pay nothing for it today." He is leading the Series A and joining the board. His other post is a Dreamforce travel joke — Lyft bikes beat sitting in a Waymo or Uber, because "just like open source models you get 90% of the value for 10% of the time!"

FPV Ventures 合伙人 Nikunj Kothari 有实质内容的那条是一份投资备忘录，而其中的论点属于支付结构，不是 AI 故事。Piston 的做法是彻底去掉加油卡："它把支付搬到自己的轨道上，不走卡组织、不经过任何中介。Piston 先付给加油站，再向车队开票。"理由是欺诈和对账属于卡这种载体本身的性质，而不是卡上的 bug——"很多卡甚至没有绑定到具体司机。它们经常被偷、被侧录、被滥用。车队老板得自己吞下欺诈损失，然后再花很长时间去核对到底发生了什么。"而每一笔交易都"绑定到具体司机，附带时间、地点和油品类型"。他给的数字：支付量"增长 8 倍"、商户网络"增长 40 倍"、留存"保持在 98% 以上"、已覆盖"2,000 座加油站、48 个州"，车队"目前一分钱不用付"。他领投了这轮 A 轮并进入董事会。他的另一条是 Dreamforce 出行玩笑——骑 Lyft 单车比堵在 Waymo 或 Uber 里快得多，因为"就像开源模型一样，你用 10% 的时间拿到 90% 的价值！"

The Takeaway

Today's feed is about scale, and two people put numbers on it from opposite ends. Aaron Levie says agents will handle "100X more volume" of background work than a single prompting session ever did, then immediately discounts it: "We're probably 1% of the way into the shape of what all these agents look like, where they get deployed from, how they get managed, how we budget for them." That gap between the volume arriving and the operating model not existing yet is the actual story.

The responses on offer are all about infrastructure rather than models. Rauch's line — "Agents are only as good as the proof-checkers, compilers, type systems, and linters you give them," and "verifiers + skills are the new 'frameworks'" — plus the Steren hire to build compute primitives "designed for" agents, puts the bottleneck one layer below the model. Levie's second post puts it one layer above: the 100X access problem is a governance one, and his framing of the trade-off is the sharpest in the feed — too much access and you cannot protect the data, lock it all down and you get no productivity gain.

Matt Turck's prisoner's dilemma post is the reason none of this gets solved by agreement. "Too many players, too many economic incentives and too much prisoner's dilemma, both domestically and globally" is an argument about structure, not about bad actors. Against that, the smaller signals still matter: Sottiaux's team shipping at DevDay volume while asking users what feature to delete, and Claude Mods landing with a Tetris implementation as its first community artifact.

核心结论

今天的 feed 讲的是规模，而且有两个人从两端各给出了一个数字。Aaron Levie 说 agent 会承接"比单次提示会话多 100 倍"的后台工作量，紧接着又自己打了折："我们大概只走完了 1%——关于这些 agent 长什么样、从哪里被部署、怎么被管理、怎么给它们做预算。"真正的新闻不是体量，而是这个落差：体量已经在路上，而运营这套体量的方式还不存在。

今天给出的答案全在基础设施层，不在模型层。Rauch 那句——"agent 的上限，取决于你给它配的证明检查器、编译器、类型系统和 linter"，以及"verifiers + skills 就是新的‘框架’"——再加上把 Steren 招进来做"为 agent 设计"的计算原语，把瓶颈压到了模型下面一层。Levie 的第二条则把瓶颈抬到模型上面一层：100 倍的访问量是个治理问题，而他给出的取舍表述是 feed 里最锋利的——给太多权限就没法保护数据，全锁死就拿不到生产力。

Matt Turck 那条囚徒困境，说明了为什么这些都不会靠共识解决。"玩家太多，经济激励太多，囚徒困境太重——国内如此，全球也如此"，这是关于结构的论证，不是关于谁的论证。在这个背景下，那些更小的信号依然值得看：Sottiaux 的团队一边以 DevDay 的密度发版本，一边问用户该删掉哪个功能；Claude Mods 上线的第一个社区产物是俄罗斯方块。
