Aaron Levie CEO, Box

Levie makes the demand-side case in one breath: "The world is going to be using coding agents for far more than anyone would have thought before" — new tool categories, software for companies that couldn't afford it, agents defending against cyber risk, automating research in life sciences, legacy system upgrades, and "hundreds of other use cases." His mechanism is simple: lower the cost of code and you do far more with it. Then he supplies the counterweight to his own optimism. The gap between AI capability and measured GDP impact is explained by diffusion, not by capability: "getting data prepared and put into a pipeline, process reengineering and change management, aligning on how the new workflow should function." And even after all that, you are still bound by "the speed of the real world."

Levie 一口气讲完了需求侧的逻辑："世界使用 coding agent 的广度，会远远超出任何人此前的想象"——全新的工具类目、给此前负担不起的公司做软件、用 agent 抵御网络风险、自动化生命科学的研究、升级遗留系统与基础设施，以及"上百种其他用例"。他的机制很简单：把代码的成本降下来，能用它做的事就会多得多。紧接着他给自己的乐观加了一个配重。AI 能力与 GDP 可测影响之间的落差，要由扩散速度解释，而不是能力本身："把数据准备好并接进管道、流程再造和变更管理、就新流程该怎么运作达成一致。"而即便这些都解决了，你依然受制于"现实世界的速度"。

Dan Shipper CEO, Every

Shipper takes apart a widely circulated automation-and-jobs report on its underlying assumptions. Two of them do the heavy lifting: that jobs can be decomposed into tasks, and that human tasks created by automation are always modelled as a fraction of automated ones — in other words, "the underlying assumption is automation always reduces human labor (or at best leaves it the same)." His objection is from experience: "automation often creates multiples more work" than it removes, and once you relax that assumption the report's conclusions move significantly. His other post is one line of workplace affection — "i love looking at the @every slack."

Shipper 拆解了一份流传很广的自动化与就业报告的底层假设。其中两个假设承担了主要推演：一是工作可以被拆成任务；二是自动化所创造的人类任务，始终被建模为自动化任务的一个分数——也就是说，"底层假设是自动化总会减少人类劳动（最多只是保持不变）"。他的反驳来自经验："自动化常常创造出数倍于此的新工作"，而一旦放松这个假设，报告的结论就会大幅移动。另一条帖子是一句对工作场所的喜爱："我喜欢看 @every 的 Slack。"

Takeaway

No Podcast Today
The Takeaway Capacity is still the story, and it is now visible in the small print. OpenAI spent the day on the unglamorous side of scale — a banked-resets bug in ChatGPT Work and Codex, a make-good to everyone in the affected window, and Altman's flat promise to prioritize service "until we can get back on top of things." While the plumbing absorbs the attention, the commercial layer keeps compounding underneath it: Anthropic opened Claude Marketplace to CrowdStrike, Cursor, Factory, Gamma and Vercel and let enterprises draw down existing spend commitments; Vercel counted eight price cuts and sixteen model discounts in six months. Levie supplies both the demand thesis — cheaper code means hundreds of new use cases — and the brake on it, which is that diffusion runs on data pipelines, process reengineering and the speed of the real world, none of which scale like a model does.
核心结论 主线仍然是产能，而且它现在出现在小字里。OpenAI 这一天都花在规模不体面的那一面：ChatGPT Work 与 Codex 里已入账 resets 未生效的故障、给受影响时间窗内所有人的补偿，以及 Altman 那句不带修饰的承诺——在"重新掌控局面之前"优先保服务。管道吸走注意力的同时，商业层在下面持续加码：Anthropic 把 Claude Marketplace 开放给 CrowdStrike、Cursor、Factory、Gamma 和 Vercel，并允许企业动用已有的支出承诺；Vercel 半年内数出 8 次降价和 16 个模型折扣。Levie 同时提供了需求侧论点——代码更便宜意味着上百种新用例——和它的刹车：扩散速度取决于数据管道、流程再造和现实世界的速度，这几样没有一样能像模型那样指数级扩张。
