Latent Space
The Revolution vs The Renaissance — Thariq Shihipar, OpenAI

The Takeaway: AI is both an industrial revolution and a renaissance, the guest argues. The revolution made software cheaper, more reliable and easier to produce at scale, and it also produces a lot of slop; the renaissance is about spending the productivity gain on something personal and on marginal gains in quality, with a human in the loop. His advice is to get the feedback loop back: build ephemeral tools, try them, and let taste come from actually using what you make.

This is Latent Space, one of a series of episodes recorded at the recent AIE Code Summit and released over the coming week. The host introduces the guest as Tariq Schipkar, most recently of Anthropic, who as of the recording had announced he was joining OpenAI; the episode title carries the spelling Thariq Shihipar. He gave the closing keynote at AIE Code. The episode is sponsored by Jira by Atlassian. Names, figures and spellings follow the transcript, and the claims in this interview are the speakers’.

He opens by listing ways he was wrong over the past year. He says he was early on Claude making videos with code and on the unreasonable effectiveness of HTML, but wrong to be Bash is all you need and React is all you need pilled: as models get more general they work in the ways people work, including using a computer to draw a front end inside Figma. His current interest is the experience of working with the model. Building a game left him disoriented because the model made assumptions he did not know about, so he made a /human folder, a scripting language whose linting rules demand a high density of comments, no single character variable names and limited line length. The files read like specs but are real code he can play with, so the model makes the underlying primitive and he changes the details, such as a character firing three firewalls at the same time, as the transcript spells it. He notes that models are good at defaults, a useful starting point but not the end.

He describes two game projects. One is a game he calls Avatar, non copyright infringing, for which he plans a WebAssembly engine with a lighter scripting layer on top and automated balance reports. He says a perfectly balanced game is boring and that variance, in the roguelike style of random choices when you level up, is what makes a game interesting. The other is a board and card game called Frontier Lab, with economic, scientific, cultural and other victory conditions; he had Claude run thousands of simulated games and found that economic victory always won. Asked to fix the balance, he says Claude was bad at it, which he treats as an eval of whether a model understands a system played over thousands of turns.

On what keeps him busy, he says he is not a permanent underclass believer and has not hit the limit on his 200 dollar plan; he is not bottlenecked by tokens or intelligence, but by ambition and by ways of working with the model. He is trying RL and interpretability experiments, such as editing Qwen so that it thinks the Eiffel Tower is in Rome, and talks about DPO, RLVR and an idea he calls Turing test RL, asking where the objective function should come from when a model could simply sound human. He says harnesses underuse decision models, which TypeSafe calls system one models: write out the behaviour in advance but evaluate the game state and decide at run time, as in a support bot. He argues a single forward pass already contains a great deal of information, using Ilya’s mystery novel example, and that the open question is how the confidence is calibrated, because a decision model can be confidently wrong.

He wants his Dot, Duet and Grok bot to talk to each other and says he hooked them all up to a TXT file as a shared scratchpad in about 30 seconds by telling one of them to introduce themselves. He dislikes the trend of everyone copying everyone else because it is hard for a consumer to tell the versions apart. On personal software he says it is hard to know what you actually want, and that cheap prototypes help: he did not keep using an alternative macOS dock that combined the dock and focus states, and is now trying a combination of to do list, screen time manager and go to sleep app. He mentions live widgets, home screen previews, iMessage activities and Shortcuts, including using a shortcut as a proxy for old smart lamps that no longer work with Siri.

Looking back, he says 2026 was the year AI worked and agents worked, with adoption faster than anything before and a dramatic change in the numbers in Anthropic’s public filings, but that software is still hard and there is no 10 percent GDP growth or major labour market effect. He expects gradual change: enterprises cannot become truly AI native, while frontier capabilities in compute, biology, robotics and energy open new revenue. On recursive self improvement he says the field has moved from auto research to optimising inference, and that pre training and post training are next; he thinks compute allocation and which experiments to run should stay with people, using speculative decoding as a thought experiment for the kind of creative leap a model might not make on its own. He describes RSI as turning AI research and fields like biology into knowledge work, with the hard technical work automated and more white collar jobs as a result. He closes on games as a prosthetic for achievement, from WoW guild leadership to IOI and IMO, token spend and AIE talks, noting that school has perfect information while adult life has fog of war.

核心结论：嘉宾认为 AI 同时是一场工业革命和一次文艺复兴。革命让软件更便宜、更可靠、更容易规模化生产，同时也制造大量 slop；文艺复兴是把生产率收益花在个人的东西上、花在每一分边际质量上，并让人留在回路里。他的建议是把反馈回路拿回来：做一次性的工具，真的去试， taste 来自实际使用自己做出来的东西。

这是 Latent Space 的一期，属于在不久前的 AIE Code Summit 录制、接下来一周陆续放出的系列节目。主持人介绍嘉宾是 Tariq Schipkar，此前在 Anthropic，录制当时刚宣布加入 OpenAI；节目标题用的是 Thariq Shihipar 这个拼法。他在 AIE Code 做了闭幕演讲。本集由 Jira by Atlassian 赞助。人名、数字和拼写按转写原样照录，访谈中的说法都属于说话人本人。

他先列了过去一年里自己判断错的地方。他说自己很早就看到 Claude 能用代码做视频、也很早就讲 HTML 不合常理的有效性，但错在曾经认定 Bash is all you need 和 React is all you need：模型越通用，就越能按人工作的方式来工作，比如用电脑在 Figma 里画前端。他现在关心的是和人一起用模型的体验。做游戏时，模型做了一些他不知道的假设，让他很迷惑，于是他做了一个 /human 文件夹，这是一种脚本语言，lint 规则要求大量注释、不用单字符变量名、限制行长度。文件读起来像 spec，但都是真代码，他可以改：模型做底层原语，他调细节，比如让角色一次打出三个 firewalls，按转写原词照录。他说模型很擅长默认值，这是很好的起点，但不是终点。

他讲了两个游戏项目。一个是他称为 Avatar 的游戏，非版权侵权，计划把引擎写成 WebAssembly，上面加一层更轻的脚本层，并自动出平衡性报告。他说一个完全平衡的游戏很无聊，真正让游戏有意思的是方差，类似 roguelike 升级时给三个随机选项。另一个是叫 Frontier Lab 的桌面卡牌游戏，有经济、科技、文化等胜利条件；他让 Claude 跑了几千局模拟，发现经济胜利总是赢。让 Claude 修平衡性，他说效果很差，他把这当作一个评测：看模型是否理解一个跑几千回合的系统。

关于现在什么让他忙，他说自己不是永久底层论者，200 美元套餐也没用完额度；他不缺 token，也不太缺智能，缺的是野心和与模型协作的方式。他在试 RL 和可解释性实验，比如改 Qwen 让它以为埃菲尔铁塔在罗马；他谈到 DPO、RLVR，以及一个他称为 Turing test RL 的想法：如果模型只要听起来像人就行，目标函数该从哪里来。他说 harness 对 decision model 用得太少，TypeSafe 把它们叫 system one model：预先写好行为，但在运行时评估游戏状态再做决定，客服机器人就是例子。他认为一次前向传播里已经包含大量信息，举了 Ilya 的推理小说例子，真正的问题是置信度怎么校准，因为 decision model 也可能自信地错。

他希望自己的 Dot、Duet 和 Grok bot 能互相说话，说他让其中一个先自我介绍，大约 30 秒就把它们全接到了一个 TXT 文件上当共享便签。他不喜欢大家互相抄的趋势，因为消费者很难分清各个版本。关于个人软件，他说很难知道自己到底想要什么，便宜的原型有帮助：他做过一个把 dock 和专注状态结合起来的 macOS 替代 dock，后来没继续用；现在在试待办清单、屏幕时间管理和助眠应用的组合。他提到 live widgets、主屏预览、iMessage activities 和快捷指令，包括用快捷指令给不再支持 Siri 的旧智能灯做代理。

回看过去，他说 2026 是 AI 真的能用、agent 真的能用的一年，采用速度快过以往任何东西，Anthropic 公开文件里的数字变化很大；但软件依然难做，也没有出现 10% 的 GDP 增长或劳动力市场的重大变化。他预期渐进式变化：企业没法真正 AI native，而计算、生物、机器人、能源这些前沿能力会打开新收入。关于递归自我改进，他说领域已经从 auto research 走到优化推理，接下来是预训练与后训练（pre-training / post-training）；他认为算力分配和跑哪些实验应该留在人手里，并用 speculative decoding 作为思想实验，说明模型自己未必能做出那种创造性跳跃。他把 RSI 描述成把 AI 研究和生物这类领域变成知识工作，难的技术部分被自动化，结果是更多白领工作。他最后谈游戏作为成就的义肢，从魔兽公会会长到 IOI、IMO、token 消耗和 AIE 演讲，并指出学校有完全信息，而成年生活有战争迷雾。
