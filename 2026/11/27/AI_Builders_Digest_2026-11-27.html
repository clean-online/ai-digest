Aaron Levie CEO, Box

Aaron Levie posts once, a quote post at the end of the window. He argues coding gives a window into what knowledge work will look like: most jobs move from doing research, typing and value creation manually to coordinating the work agents do, then to giving agents goals to optimize as that coordination layer becomes redundant. He says a large share of knowledge work may converge on this form because of the productivity gains, with agents using computers, existing or new tools, and code written on the fly; he names manufacturing, architecture, life sciences, material sciences and cyber. He flags that much of this work is non-verifiable, so humans will be more in the loop than in coding, though the handoff grows as models improve. His summary is to watch coding to see where work is going (12 likes).

Aaron Levie 在窗口末尾发了一条引用推文。他认为编程让人看到知识工作会变成什么样：大多数工作会从手动做研究、打字和创造价值，转向协调 agent 做这些事；等这个协调层变得多余或拖慢 agent，人们就会给 agent 设定要优化的目标。他说很大一部分知识工作可能因为生产率收益而收敛到这个形态，agent 会用电脑、用现成或新写的工具、即时写代码解决问题；他点名制造、建筑、生命科学、材料科学和网络安全。他提醒其中很多工作不可验证，所以人会比在编程里更深地参与其中，尽管能交给 agent 的部分会随模型进步变大。他的总结是：盯着编程，就能看到工作往哪走（12 赞）。

OpenAI’s Noam Brown: Why Game Theory Didn’t Lead to General Intelligence (Live at No Limit Finals)

The Takeaway: the path to general intelligence did not run through game theory. Brown says game theory rests on all players being rational and that being common knowledge, and people are not rational; what worked was training on human data, modelling human behaviour, and scaling reasoning through reinforcement learning on chain of thought and longer test-time compute. He calls the current stretch the centaur period, in which human plus AI works best because models are jagged, strong at some things and weak at others.

This is Training Data, recorded live at the No Limit finals, with OpenAI’s Noam Brown. The host walks through a path from poker to game theory and diplomacy to reasoning. Brown says he started on poker AI in grad school in 2012, drawn by game theory and economics and by the idea that poker had a perfect strategy nobody knew. He says he won the MIT PokerBots competition two years in a row, in 2014 and 2015, with a friend at MIT. Asked how he would build a bot now, he says the approaches he took were GTO oriented: self play with CFR, later neural self play, and that training took many hours. He says that for this tournament format an exploitation based approach that reads opponents may be better than GTO, and that recent general models have got good enough at poker that GPT-6 Astra can make money playing online, in his telling.

On diplomacy he says the aim was a general technique, not one that only works on a narrow domain, because poker bots did not transfer well to chess or Go. Diplomacy was chosen as the hardest case: a natural language game of negotiation, alliances and both cooperation and conflict. He says the game theoretic premise of common knowledge of rationality makes bots misread people, and uses the ultimatum game as an example: the game theoretic move is to offer a penny, but a person offered a penny refuses. His team found their diplomacy bots drifting toward vengeance and fairness behaviours, and concluded that the way to a superhuman diplomacy agent was to train models to imitate people, including their irrationality, and then train the agent against those imitation humans.

That led to what he calls a crisis of faith around 2022. His whole career had been computational game theory, but the evidence pointed instead to human data, modelling people, and scaled reasoning. He chose to work on what he judged most promising and joined OpenAI, working on what became o1. He recalls that in 2022 many people expected pretraining scale alone to solve the remaining problems, while he argued that reaching competent Go play by scaling would take roughly six orders of magnitude and was not realistic. The decisive idea was to make models think before acting: chain of thought prompting was coarse, but reinforcement learning trained on it produced much better behaviour, and he reports that the longer the model thinks, the better it does. He calls this the key unlock for scaling test time compute beyond games.

On what humans still add, Brown says models are complements rather than substitutes for now. They are strong at some tasks and poor at others, including long term planning and noticing that they are heading the wrong way; as an example he says a model asked to implement CFR can spend hours perfecting its numerics when CFR is robust to small numerical error. He says 5.5 and 5.6 were the first models he felt were ten to twenty times his own ability to build a poker bot, while Astra can produce an excellent river solver from one prompt but not yet a full scale poker bot. On chain of thought he says it is a somewhat odd way to scale test time compute and may not be the best solution, but it is sufficient, and notes that people sometimes think in images, which chain of thought does not capture.

On recursive self improvement he says it is real but not new: all of human technological progress is a single RSI loop, from telescopes to punch cards to compilers and LLMs, with people shifting to what the technology is still bad at. What differs now is the generality and speed, and he expects a steep rate of progress over the next year or two; his most controversial remark is that a promising student should drop out and join a lab now, pointing to dropouts he has hired who did well. In the lightning round he says he cannot think of a game that needs a bespoke agent, that games are better used as evaluations for general LLMs, and that current models cannot play real time strategy or Zelda at a superhuman level; asked whether one AI could beat all humans at the World Series of Poker within five years, he says a GTO agent probably could not because of variance, but gives roughly a 20% chance if it can read opponents well enough. Names, figures and spellings follow the transcript; this is an interview and its claims are the speakers’.

核心结论：通向通用智能的路并没有经过博弈论。Brown 说博弈论的前提是所有玩家理性、且这是共同知识，但人并不理性；真正起作用的是在人类数据上训练、建模人的行为，再用强化学习训练思维链、拉长测试时计算来扩展推理。他把当下称为半人马时期，人加 AI 的组合最有效，因为模型能力是参差不齐的：有些事很强，有些事很弱。

这是 Training Data 在 No Limit 决赛现场录制的一期，嘉宾是 OpenAI 的 Noam Brown。主持人带他回顾了从扑克到博弈论、外交，再到推理的路径。Brown 说 2012 年读研时开始做扑克 AI，吸引他的是博弈论和经济学，以及扑克存在一套没人知道的完美策略这个想法。他说自己和一个 MIT 的朋友连续两年赢得 MIT PokerBots 比赛，是 2014 和 2015 年。被问到现在会怎么做 bot，他说自己过去用的方法以 GTO 为主：用 CFR 自我对弈，后来换成神经网络自我对弈，训练要花很多小时。他说在这种赛制下，靠读对手的利用式打法可能比 GTO 更好；又说近期的通用模型在扑克上已经够强，按他的说法 GPT-6 Astra 现在能在网上扑克赚钱。

谈到外交，他说目标是找到通用方法，而不是只在一个窄领域有效的技术，因为扑克机器人并不能很好地迁移到国际象棋或围棋。外交被选作最难的例子：一个自然语言游戏，涉及谈判、结盟，既有合作也有对抗。他说博弈论关于理性是共同知识的前提会让机器人误判人，并用最后通牒游戏举例：博弈论的做法是只给一分钱，但真人被给一分钱会拒绝。他的团队发现外交机器人开始出现报复和追求公平的行为，于是结论是，要做超人水平的外交 agent，得先训练模型模仿人、包括人的非理性，再让 agent 和这些模仿人的模型对抗训练。

这带来他所说的 2022 年前后的信仰危机。他的整个职业生涯都是计算博弈论，但证据指向的却是人类数据、建模人，以及扩展推理。他选择做自己判断最有价值的方向，加入 OpenAI，参与了后来成为 o1 的工作。他回忆 2022 年很多人觉得单靠预训练规模就能解决剩下的问题，而他认为要让模型达到能下围棋的水平，大概需要六个数量级的规模扩张，并不现实。关键思路是让模型在行动前先思考：思维链提示很粗糙，但用强化学习训练这种思维链行为后，效果好得多；他说模型思考得越久，表现越好。他把这称为把测试时计算扩展到游戏之外的关键突破。

关于人还剩什么价值，Brown 说目前模型是人的补充而不是替代。它们在某些任务上很强，在另一些上很差，包括长期规划，以及意识到自己走错了方向；他举例说，让模型实现 CFR，它可能花几个小时纠结数值精度，而 CFR 对小数值误差本来就很鲁棒。他说 5.5 和 5.6 是他第一次觉得模型把自己做扑克机器人的能力提升了十到二十倍，Astra 单靠一条提示能做出很好的河牌求解器，但还做不出完整规模的扑克机器人。关于思维链，他说这是扩展测试时计算的一种有点奇怪的方式，未必是最好的方案，但已经够用；他也提到人有时用图像思考，而思维链捕捉不到这一点。

关于递归自我改进，他说这事真实存在但并不新鲜：人类全部技术进步就是一个大的 RSI 循环，从望远镜到打孔卡、编译器和大模型，人不断转向技术还不擅长的部分。现在不同的是通用性和速度，他预计未来一两年进展会很快；他最具争议的一句话是，有前途的学生应该现在就辍学加入实验室，并举了自己招过的、表现很好的辍学生为例。在快问快答里，他说想不出哪个游戏还需要专门做一个 agent，游戏更适合作为通用大模型的评测，而当下的模型还不能在实时战略游戏或塞尔达上达到超人水平。被问到五年内单个 AI 能否在世界扑克大赛上击败所有人类，他说走 GTO 的 agent 大概不行，因为方差太大，但如果能足够好地读对手，他给大约 20% 的胜率。人名、数字和拼写按转写原样照录；这是一场访谈，其中的说法都属于说话人本人。
