Vlad Tenev, founder and CEO of Robinhood, frames the company in one word: ownership. He argues it matters twice over, because owning assets is the tried-and-true route to financial independence for an individual, and because a society without broad ownership is fragile, while people who own things become defenders of the system. He traces that instinct to childhood in Bulgaria at the collapse of the Soviet Union, with 170% inflation in 1991 and 2,000% annualized in 1997, rolling blackouts, and a grandfather who converted his salary into copper cookware as a store of value. Robinhood now operates in some form in over 120 countries, and he lists Trump accounts, Robinhood Ventures and tokenization as extensions of the same idea, the last being “ownership gone global”.

On the math side he is blunt about the current wave being too centralized. Harmonic, a startup he co-founded to build mathematical superintelligence, produced Aristotle, which he says was one of three models to get a gold medal on the International Math Olympiad about a year ago, and was later opened to the public, where people used it on unsolved problems including Erdosche problems. Math, he says, barely changed for thousands of years and has now converged with coding through version control and Lean. His objection is to a world where a few AI companies own the data centers, the secret models and every discovery, and he calls that a dystopian outcome that would be far better received if the tools were put in the hands of ordinary users. He does not claim this is inevitable; he says it is unlikely for reasons beyond just technological and market ones.

On products, tokenization wraps a real asset in a box and mints tokens against it, the way a stablecoin wraps dollars or treasuries. Robinhood launched stock tokens in Europe last year without full DeFi support and relaunched them this year fully DeFi enabled alongside Robinhood Chain, a blockchain built for real world assets that he calls one of the fastest growing across a lot of metrics in history and the number one chain by developer activity. He answers the corpo chain criticism by saying a little organizational decentralization purity is a fair trade for focused leadership and backed primitives. On prediction markets he calls them an evolution of news, where the odds show both what is happening and what is likely to happen. He describes KPI contracts as announced and planned for launch, and says they now allow trading an EPS or revenue print directly instead of using the stock as a proxy; the transcript uses both tenses and the feed does not reconcile them.

The agentic trading numbers are all his own characterisations, with no external check in the feed. He says over 150,000 customers have opened agentic trading accounts in the few months since launch, and that tool calls have followed a smooth exponential to something like 30,000,000 per day as last announced. The product began as a Robinhood MCP, and at the Hood Summit a couple of weeks earlier became Robinhood Agents, with the harness and UI inside a Robinhood account, plus skills and an agent app store that can connect to sources such as satellite data. His north star is putting the capabilities of a hedge fund or a sophisticated algorithmic trading firm into the pockets of normal individuals. He says agentic trades are still a low percentage of total trading and not imminently a double-digit percentage, so there is warning time, and the day-one safeguards are a segregated account, trade approvals on by default, and a sandbox he says is close to air gapped.

On the ownership gap he says the major AI companies are private, so individuals could not get exposure to OpenAI or Anthropic a few years ago, and an AI lab listing at a trillion or two would leave no room for the 1,000x returns that Microsoft or Amazon IPO buyers once had.
