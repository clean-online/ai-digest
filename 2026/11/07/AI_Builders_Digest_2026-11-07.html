Ep 97: Jev Creator on Decision Models, Benchmark Fatigue & Why the Labs Missed It

The most portable idea is a refusal. “I’m anti benchmarks.” He says people have already stopped caring about new releases in his class of model because they have been lied to so many times, that benchmarks do not capture reliability, and that shipping the shape of a task without utility is “pure overpromised, underdelivered” that burns the goodwill developers need. His north star is reliability, not cost or speed: “Why can we solve Millennium Prize problems in math but not automate anything touching accounting?” He wants intelligence to be “a lot like SQL”, so reliable that it is provider agnostic, and says JEV is not there yet.

The name Jev comes from Jevons paradox, which the transcript writes as “Jevan’s paradox”. He says the rebound effect is real, cheaper intelligence gets thrown at more places, but backfire, a larger total market, has not happened yet: “Absolutely not.” The one estimate with numbers is coding agents: a 20 to 40% speedup, with error bars for long-term effects like skill degradation and tech debt, depending on the use case and the user’s skill; some uses are “kind of like an infinite percent speed up”, others as bad as coding by hand, and on average “a decent but small boost”. On images he puts his own model at “maybe 10% of the way that our text model is”, calls it completely insufficient and says he would be embarrassed to call it prod ready. These are his characterizations, not measurements.

The longest technical thread is what he calls the tyranny of the KV cache: nearly every coding-agent design decision is built around it, state becomes an append-only global dictionary, and this blocks effective routing and sub-agents. He floats alternatives, multiple agents sharing one context, semantic compaction, a cheap MapReduce over history, semantic dependent typing and semantic security checks on every tool call, and labels them a spoiler for a future release and “cool sci fi”, not shipped features. He also says the field is in a “Stockholm syndrome” where developers wait for model providers instead of pushing them, and that real-world usage feeds back into the roadmap “almost none”.

On Anthropic’s IPO he declines a valuation call: expectations are miscalibrated because some people value “building god as basically infinite”, and “the let them cure cancer is going to be like Marie Antoinette’s, let them eat cake”. His sharpest claim about the labs is that their greatest strength is not models, product or distribution but financial engineering, especially on compute deals, and that a bubble can pop simply because expectations are missed, not because the technology stops being transformative. On safety he separates ASI, bio, cyber and trust-and-safety into different threat models, estimates true agency is two breakthroughs of gigantic magnitude away, and says treating Bostrom’s Superintelligence as reality rather than interesting science fiction worries him. The host phrases Anthropic as a “$1.52000000000000 dollar company”; the transcript gives no unit or magnitude and it is not reconstructed here.

He closes by asking the field to unlearn the lack of agency LLMs have taught it and “get back to playing rather than being these victims of stuff”, and to build “playable AI”. A producer read in the middle of the transcript, Anne Maris with a 35,000 subscriber target by year end, is a mid-roll spot and not a guest position.
