AI & I by Every
Notion’s Founder on the One Thing Your AI Is Missing

The Takeaway: a product built to let anyone make software without code now has to decide what it is for, and Ivan Zhao's answer is that the durable part is the data, not the interface. The founder and CEO of Notion traces the company to the 1960s and 1970s idea of software as a medium that everyone should be able to read and write, like prose. Vibe coding finally makes that possible, which he loves and which also forced Notion to rethink its original vision. The episode is a conversation between Dan Shipper and Ivan Zhao, introduced at the top by Dan Shipper and Natalia Quintero.

Zhao separates the question into three buckets. If you want to modify the tools you use, his answer is 'hell yes, definitely': that is basic freedom with a medium. But for things you use every day and have no strong opinion about, a chair, a car, a door, a calendar, you want someone to work out the best form factor and just do a good job, and 'just because you can doesn't mean you should'. He mentions a second version of the Notion Calendar app on mobile, built from scratch and native. Enterprise software you depend on for security is a third bucket he would not recommend self-building. Underneath all of it, he argues that AI agents can write the procedural code and the UI for any workflow, so what is durable is how you hold your data, your context and your semantics; Notion's job is becoming the keeper of that data, open to different agents (the transcript says 'contacts'), with a guardrail and a place for a human team to collaborate.

He breaks Notion's 13 years into chapters: five years of meandering in the dark, product-market fit and growth during COVID, then a turn at the end of 2022 when early access to a GPT-4 class model convinced him the world would be different. He and his co-founder locked themselves in a hotel room to build the first prototype and launched a product a couple of weeks before ChatGPT appeared. The rest of the company was harder to bring along: people read AI as a fad, like crypto, after so many waves. A writing product found traction in the first six months; an agent product took two hard years because the model was not there and only started working around this time last year, by which point everybody was building agents. That sent the company back to people, UI, context and data.

On second-order effects he is unusually concrete. He notices he would rather chat with his claw than read on his Kindle, and that the pattern sometimes feels like TikTok. Asked about a rule that would have an agent ask, after 11 at night and several hours of chatting, that you explain what you are asking it to build, he calls that too superficial: the deeper question is whether the human still understands the system. 'I don't think we should surrender our understanding. Definitely not without putting up a fight.' His example of a fix is a shared Notion Kanban board instead of each person talking to a private coding agent, an old visual metaphor that turns out to work for orchestrating a group of agents.

A question about structure versus unstructured draws the Lego-and-clay answer. Vibe coding hands you clay, JavaScript and fine-grained pieces, but a maker often lacks the opinion or expertise to build a tall tower; the industry is moving toward what the Legos of vibe coding are, with permissions, syncing and collaboration handled for you. He says Notion pattern libraries now open up to agents and can read from Notion databases under Notion system permissions, and when asked whether the library is open source he answers 'We haven't open source yet'. For a 50-person company he sets a goal of the next quarter, 'less than two, hopefully', for an out-of-the-box context and knowledge system onboarded through an agent, and asks whether a 50-person startup could save 20%, 30% or 40% of tokens if content, which he calls compressed tokens, flows through the system, saying it might be possible. He says that, if everything holds true, hundreds of billions of dollars will probably be spent through tokens in the next year or two.

On the idea that people will stop using apps and let AI use them, he says it is not black and white: inside Notion they assume that of the next billion knowledge workers the vast majority will be agents, so the customer is no longer only human, but human camaraderie and collaboration still matter and he does not want a world where everyone just chats with a box. What he cares about is a shared canvas where a team of humans works with a team of agents, part of which is closer to a headless product with no UI at all. He also describes a history game he builds on the side, with a PlayStation 2 look he finds more approachable and easy to tweak, and a voice model he and his wife tried that felt too creepy and was replaced by multiple choice. The cold open repeats a line from the talk, a mid-roll Lovable read is an ad, and Notion sponsored the event; none is treated as an argument. 核心结论：一个为了让不懂代码的人也能做软件而生的产品，现在要重新想自己到底做什么。Notion 创始人兼 CEO Ivan Zhao 给的答案是，耐久的是数据，不是界面。他把公司源头追到 1960、70 年代那个「软件是一种媒介、人人都该能读写」的想法，像写散文一样。vibe coding 终于让它成为可能，他既喜欢这一点，也说正是它逼 Notion 重新想创立时的愿景。这一集是 Dan Shipper 与 Ivan Zhao 的对话，片头由 Dan Shipper 和 Natalia Quintero 引入。

Zhao 把问题分成三类。想改自己每天用的工具，他的回答是 'hell yes, definitely'，这是使用一种媒介的基本自由；但像椅子、汽车、门、日历这类你没有强观点、只想有人替你弄好的东西，'just because you can doesn't mean you should'。他提到当天发布的第二版移动端 Notion Calendar app，从零构建、原生。第三类是依赖安全性的企业软件，他不建议自己搭。贯穿三类的判断是：AI agent 能写任意 workflow 的流程代码和 UI，所以耐久的是你怎么保存数据、context 和语义；Notion 的工作越来越是当这些数据的保管者，向不同 agent 开放（转写写作 'contacts'），提供 guardrail，并给人类团队留出协作的位置。

他把 Notion 的 13 年拆成几段：前五年在黑暗里摸索，COVID 期间找到 product-market fit 并开始增长，2022 年底因为拿到 GPT-4 级模型的早期访问而确信世界会变。他和联合创始人把自己锁在酒店房间做第一个原型，在 ChatGPT 出现前几周就上线了产品。把公司其他人带过来更难：AI 经历过太多波浪，当时很多人把它当成像 crypto 一样的 fad。最初六个月的 writing product 有了 traction，之后的 agent product 因为模型没到而熬了两年，直到去年这个时候才跑通，而那时大家都在做 agent。这又把公司推回人、UI、context 和数据。

谈二阶效应时他讲得很具体。他发现自己更愿意跟 claw 聊，而不是在 Kindle 上读书，有时觉得这个模式像 TikTok。主持人举例说，能不能让 agent 在夜里 11 点、聊了几个小时后要求你先解释要做什么、解释不出就别做；他说这太表层，更深层的是人还理不理解这个系统：'I don't think we should surrender our understanding. Definitely not without putting up a fight.' 他给的一个解法是改用共享的 Notion Kanban board，而不是各人对着自己的 coding agent 小黑盒，一个老的视觉隐喻结果很适合编排一群 agent。

有观众问结构化与非结构化，他给了 Lego 和 clay 的回答。vibe coding 给的是 clay、JavaScript 和细粒度零件，但制作者常常没有观点或专业能力去搭一座高塔；行业正在往「什么是 vibe coding 的 Legos」合流，权限、同步、协作替你做好。他说 Notion pattern libraries 现在向 agent 开放、能在 Notion 系统权限下读 databases；被问到是不是开源库，他答 'We haven't open source yet'。对 50 人的公司，他把目标定在 next quarter，'less than two, hopefully'，做出开箱即用、通过 agent 完成 onboarding 的 context 与知识系统；他问，如果 content（他称作 compressed tokens）能在系统里流动，50 人的 startup 能不能省下 20%、30% 或 40% 的 tokens，并说也许可能。他说如果一切成立，未来一两年可能会有 hundreds of billions dollars 花在 tokens 上。

对「以后别用 app 了，让 AI 去用 app」这个说法，他说不是非黑即白：Notion 内部假设 next billion knowledge worker 里绝大多数会是 agent，客户不再只是人类；但人与人之间的 camaraderie 和协作仍然重要，他不想活在人人只跟一个聊天框对话的世界里。他在意的是一块共享 canvas，让一群人和一群 agent 一起工作，其中一部分更接近 headless product、完全没有 UI。他还讲了自己顺手做的历史游戏，画风像 PlayStation 2，他觉得更易接近、更好改；他和太太试过加 voice model 实时回话，觉得太 creepy，改成了多选。开头的 cold open 重复了正文里的一句，中插的 Lovable 是广告，Notion 赞助了这场活动，这些都不当作独立论点。拼写按转写原样照录（Ivan Zao、Cloud Code、claw、chat.gpt、YNOTION、Chachamity、Argon）。这里每个数字都是对话里的自述，feed 没有独立核实。
