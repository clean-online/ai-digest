Latent Space
Building Visual Intelligence — Andreas Blattmann & Dustin Podell, Black Forest Labs

The Takeaway: a lab that became famous for image generation says it was never really an image generation lab. Andreas Blattmann and Dustin Podell of Black Forest Labs describe visual intelligence as the intuitive, pre-verbal grasp of physics that lets a person catch a ball or grab a bottle, and argue that learning from natural signals (images, video, audio and actions) is what turns diffusion models into something closer to a robot brain than a picture tool. The cold open puts it as BFL was not founded to be an image generation lab, and Blattmann says video modeling is actually the central means to understanding how our world basically works.

Blattmann is a co-inventor of latent diffusion, the technique scaled up into Stable Diffusion at Stability AI, and a co-founder who has been working on diffusion models for about seven years. Podell came in from freelance web development, became a model and research lead at Stability AI and is credited with SDXL, Control LoRA, post-training on SD3, Flux Ultra, Flux.1 and Flux Context. The name Black Forest comes from the German region around Freiburg where the founders grew up: they could pick where to start the lab and chose home. The host says the launch of Stable Diffusion made him quit his job and start Latent Space, and Podell says the original release made him quit his own job too.

The technical spine is FLUX 3, described as one model learning multimodally from images, videos, audio and actions, able to act as a robot brain in real manufacturing workflows. Blattmann frames the goal through Daniel Kahneman's system one, fast and intuitive, versus the system two of language modeling, and says the two should eventually be combined. Podell's account of Flux Context is the most concrete: a tiny set of photographs labeled only with spatial relations such as the hat is on top of the dog, a training run he describes as maybe six hours or four hours, and an immediate sense of generality that let a logo be placed on a shirt. He calls it mostly a data set project, with distillation work by Axel to speed it up, and says the point was to get away from the slot machine of prompting again and again. Other figures in the conversation are self-reported: $450 million raised so far, $100 million in revenue last published in December and since crossed, Flux.2 handling 10 reference images, and 1K fine-tuning steps on 3D data turning a video model into a 3D one.

The host says Flux Context could in the limit replace Canva and Photoshop for everyday edits while not replacing the top end of the market, and that when many frontier labs lean the same direction you are probably six to 12 months out from a big breakthrough; at the close he places robotics in 2027. An unexpected use case the guests liked was image restoration, bringing old family photos back. Sponsor reads for Supabase and Deepgram and the cold open are not treated as arguments.
