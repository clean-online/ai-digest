Latent Space
Using Agents for Long-Horizon Financial Work — Aman Singh, Arca

Latent Space records this one at the AI Engineer New York conference with Aman Singh, who leads the AI platform team at Arca, described on air as a wealth management firm that recently raised $64M and that is AI-native. The episode is published 2026-10-31T16:00Z, Pacific 10-31 09:00, inside this window and inside the same Pacific day.

The thesis is in two sentences, both repeated. “We’re actually not a SaaS company. We are the firm ourselves.” And: “Agents prompt people more than people prompt agents”, a service running for the client twenty-four seven. The design rule follows from it: the role of AI “is not to replace the advisor. It is to replace all of the work around the advisor.” Clients never touch the AI; what they get is a level of service the industry could not previously deliver, through a human advisor.

Models come from Anthropic and OpenAI, with Claude primary, and the firm has moved through Gemini, then OpenAI, then Anthropic. What decides is an internal benchmark set rather than external ones, and the example given is a named model: “despite the external benchmarks claiming great things for Luna, it didn’t really quite work out for us as well as we had expected. It was way too slow” and it was costing a lot. Luna is a name this series has carried in the 08-01, 08-05, 09-18, 09-24, 09-25 and 09-30 issues, every time as a price cut, a launch, or something running somewhere, and never as something that failed; the 09-24 entry has it as GPT-6 Luna alongside GPT-6 Sol with a permanent 50% API price cut. Whether that is the same model he means is not stated here. The principle he states is model-agnostic, which is why they run their own harness rather than one of the labs’.

Routing is by task complexity and is invisible to the person using it. A tax analysis goes to the heaviest tier, inside which an Opus-tier model, a Sonnet-tier model or a heavy GPT model are interchangeable; background activity runs on a lower tier. Of the advisors: “they’re not aware of that happening”, the choice having been abstracted away entirely.

The worked example spans ten days. A client emails a tax bill and needs $100,000 raised. The analysis, where the money sits and what to liquidate to get there, is fully the agent’s. The execution is not: there are “strict guardrails around human in the loop for things that would either move money or liquidate assets, or even directly communicate with our end clients”, and this is a firm that does hold custodian APIs capable of doing it automatically. The same line holds for trading: agents propose trades, and experts decide and execute them. There is no autonomous trading.

Resuming days later is handled by memory rather than by context. A fresh signal, a phone call made in the middle of a process, wakes a fresh agent that has no thread to attach to. What it reads is a per-client state: what is happening now, who owns what, what has happened so far, what is tracked for the next few days and for the long term. On top of that sits what Singh calls a “dreaming process” that reflects on the state of the world as it relates to the client and updates those memory systems, which is how a long-horizon task survives the gap.

Safety is deterministic wherever it can be. Some tools the agent executes itself, simple record-keeping for instance; every write to the database gets a fast LLM-based verification running on it; inbound email, documents and phone calls are treated as prompt-injection targets and checked at the edge; and anything leaving the system, a web search or a calendar invite to a client, passes both deterministic checks for known PII and a consensus of small models watching for exfiltration. The principle is stated as wanting to contain these “at the edges of capability” and not earlier. Compliance cares about auditability, and the answer is default-on: every action, agent or human, writes a change log of what changed, who changed it, from which part of the system and when, which is also what feeds the agentic loop.

The sandbox is used sideways. The agent is not inside it; it uses the sandbox to offload computation, and the code it writes is translated into a strict expected format, then thrown away, with guardrails put on that format before it reaches the backend. What comes out is the artifact, a PDF financial plan, and that is where the verifiers run: deterministic checks for things like a raw account number, and fast models, named as “the smaller models or JEV”, for things like promising returns. JEV has run through this series since 09-18. The claim is that this makes compliance better than a human would, because AI is tireless at finding even the smallest non-compliant phrase.

Decision models are used where the model is confidently wrong rather than where it fails. Models will happily use 2025 tax brackets and present the result as settled, and the decision model detects the moment a financial recommendation is about to be made and nudges toward a verified fact base or web search. They are explicitly not used for trade-failure recovery, which is not a case being explored. The feedback loop is the most concrete part: every staff edit or cancellation comes with a why and becomes an eval; feedback is stored coupled to the trace that produced it; and every night a meta agent runs over real-world traces and suggests harness changes, misleading tools, errors, missing knowledge. A human engineer still ships the prompt change. “Agents will make really nasty prompt changes if you leave them to it”, because every small fix becomes the whole prompt.

Two positions on Anthropic, cleanly separated. On the models, bullish. On the product, “less so on taking a dependency on a product like managed agents”, because what managed agents owns is how models persist and contribute memory and all the operational knowledge, and “owning your intelligence is kind of, uh, gives you the ability to impact the business”. Managed agents is a name this series first carried on 10-27 with no description attached; this is a buyer’s position on it, not an explanation of what it is. Anthropic moving into financial services is called “very validating”, with the line drawn between a services firm that has AI tools, where users still prompt the agents, and a firm built to be AI-native.

The one client story carries figures. A prospect, not yet a client, already paying an advisor $1,500 and unhappy with the responsiveness. The agents read the statements, found fees the person did not know they were being charged, and found that the advisor was not even a fiduciary. The other proactivity examples come without numbers: upcoming deadlines, and clients at risk of missing an RMD.

Two transcript artifacts worth flagging, since the rest of this is quoted from it: the firm is rendered as “Orka” once near the end, and the guest is thanked as “Iman”. And the url field points to the Latent Space channel page rather than the episode, as it has in earlier entries in this series.

Latent Space 这一期是在 AI Engineer New York 大会上录的，嘉宾是 Aman Singh，他在 Arca 带 AI platform 团队；节目里把 Arca 描述成一家最近融了 6,400 万美元、并且是 AI-native 的财富管理公司。这一集发布于 2026-10-31T16:00Z，即太平洋时间 10-31 09:00，落在这个窗口之内，也是同一个太平洋日。

主张是两句话，两句话都被重复过。「我们其实不是一家 SaaS 公司，我们自己就是那家公司。」以及「agent 提示人的次数，比人提示 agent 的次数多」，这是一项为客户端到端、7×24 运行的服务。设计规则由此而来：AI 的角色「不是取代顾问，而是取代顾问周围的全部工作」。客户从不直接接触 AI；他们拿到的是这个行业此前做不到的一层服务，而递给他们的是一个真人顾问。

模型来自 Anthropic 和 OpenAI，主力是 Claude，而这家公司先后用过 Gemini、然后是 OpenAI、然后是 Anthropic。做决定的是他们内部的一套 benchmark，而不是外部的；他举的例子是一个有名字的模型：「尽管外部 benchmark 把 Luna 说得很好，它对我们并没有像预期那样奏效。它太慢了」，而且成本很高。Luna 这个名字本系列在 08-01、08-05、09-18、09-24、09-25 和 09-30 这几期里都记过，每次都是降价、发布，或者某个地方在跑它，从来没有一次是「不好用」；09-24 那期记的是和 GPT-6 Sol 一起发布、带永久性 50% API 降价的 GPT-6 Luna。他指的是不是同一个模型，这里没有说明。他声称的原则是与模型无关，所以跑的是自己的 harness，而不是某家实验室的。

路由按任务复杂度走，并且对使用它的人完全不可见。税务分析走最重的那一档，在这一档内部，Opus 档、Sonnet 档或一个重型 GPT 模型可以互换；后台活动跑在更低的档位上。关于顾问，他的说法是：「他们并不知道这件事在发生」，这个选择被整套抽象掉了。

他讲的例子跨十天。客户发来一张税单，需要凑出 10 万美元。分析部分，钱在哪里、要动哪一部分才划算，完全由 agent 做。执行部分不是：「凡是会动钱、会清算资产，甚至会直接和我们终端客户沟通的事，我们都有严格的人类在环护栏」，而这是一家确实持有托管方 API、有能力自动执行的公司。交易上也是同一条线：agent 提议交易，由专家判断并执行。没有自动交易。

隔几天再续上靠的是记忆，不是上下文。一个新信号，比如过程中打进来的一通电话，会唤醒一个全新的 agent，它没有任何可以挂靠的线程。它读的是每位客户的一份状态：现在在发生什么、谁负责什么、到目前为止发生了什么、接下来几天要跟什么、长期要跟什么。在这之上还有一个他称之为 “dreaming process” 的东西，它反思与客户相关的世界状态并更新这套记忆，长周期任务就是这样跨过那段时间差的。

凡是能确定化的地方，安全就是确定化的。有些工具 agent 自己执行，比如简单的记录；每一次写数据库都会跑一次快速的、基于 LLM 的校验；进来的邮件、文件和电话都被当成 prompt injection 的目标，在边缘上检查；而任何离开系统的东西，一次网页搜索、发给客户的一个日历邀请，都要过一遍针对已知 PII 的确定性检查，外加一组小模型的共识判断有没有数据外泄。原则被表述为：要「在能力的边缘上」做约束，而不是更早。合规部门要的是可审计，而他们的答案是默认就有：每一个动作，无论来自 agent 还是人，都会写一条变更日志，记录改了什么、谁改的、来自系统的哪一部分、什么时候；这条日志同时也是驱动 agent 循环的东西。

沙箱是横着用的。agent 不在沙箱里面，它把沙箱当成卸载计算的地方；它写的代码会被翻译成一种他们期望的严格格式，然后代码就被丢掉，护栏加在那个格式上，再送到后端。产出的东西是那份工件，一份 PDF 财务规划，而校验器就跑在这里：确定性的检查负责账号裸号这类东西，快速模型负责「承诺收益」这类东西，他点名的是「更小的模型或 JEV」。JEV 从 09-18 起就出现在本系列里。他的主张是这样做出来的合规比人做的更好，因为 AI 在找出哪怕最小的一处不合规表述上是不会疲倦的。

decision model 用在模型自信地犯错的地方，而不是用在模型失败的地方。模型会很愉快地拿 2025 年的税率表算完，然后把结果当成定论；decision model 则在它即将给出财务建议的那一刻检测到，并把它推向一份经过核验的事实库或网页搜索。它们明确没有用在交易失败的恢复上，那个场景他们现在没在做。反馈闭环是讲得最具体的一段：每一次员工修改或取消都带一个 why，并且变成一条 eval；反馈被存下来，和产生它的那条 trace 绑在一起；每天晚上会有一个 meta agent 跑一遍真实世界的 trace，对 harness 提出修改建议，误导性的工具、错误、缺失的知识。最终提交 prompt 改动的仍然是一位人类工程师。「如果你放着不管，agent 会做出非常糟糕的 prompt 改动」，因为每一个小修补最后都会变成整个 prompt。

对 Anthropic 有两个立场，分得很清楚。对模型，看多。对产品，「对依赖 managed agents 这类产品就没那么积极」，因为 managed agents 掌握的是模型如何持久化和贡献记忆、以及全部运营知识，而「拥有自己的智能，会让你拥有影响业务的能力」。managed agents 这个名字本系列在 10-27 第一次记到，当时没有任何描述；这里是一个买方的立场，不是在解释它是什么。Anthropic 进入金融服务被他称为「非常有验证力」，而他划的线是：一家手上有 AI 工具的服务公司，用户仍然要去提示 agent；和一家从一开始就是按 AI-native 建起来的公司。

唯一那个客户故事是带数字的。一位还不是客户的人，已经在付给一位顾问 1,500 美元，并且不满意对方的响应速度。agent 读完了那些对账单，找到了这位客户不知道自己在被收取的费用，并且发现那位顾问甚至不是 fiduciary。其他的主动服务例子没有带数字：即将到期的截止日，以及有错过 RMD 风险的客户。
