Beam: The Great American Open Model with ReflectionAI Co-Founder and CEO Misha Laskin

Reflection AI went from roughly 30 people a year ago to roughly 300 now, and released Beam, its first open model. Misha Laskin, a DeepMind researcher with a physics PhD, describes the year as assembling a plane while flying it, and when asked what was hardest he answers that everything was hard, and that the honest answer to what made the models work is thirty things rather than one. The headcount is the only number in the opening, and the rest is a list: talent, retention, mission, data, compute, and the infrastructure that makes compute usable.

Reflection AI 从一年前大约 30 人变成现在的约 300 人，并发布了 Beam，他们的第一个开放模型。Misha Laskin 是 DeepMind 的研究员、物理学博士，他把这一年描述成“一边飞一边把飞机装起来”；被问到最难的是什么时，他的回答是全都很难，而“是什么让模型跑通了”的诚实答案是三十件事，不是一件。开场里唯一的数字是人数，其余是一份清单：人才、留存、使命、数据、算力，以及让算力真正可用的基础设施。

Beam is the most specific thing in the episode. It is a 500 billion parameter model with 23 billion active. Pretraining ran on 6,000 GB300s, originally a few weeks, which he says infrastructure work has since brought down to about twelve days. Reinforcement learning ran on a little over 10,000 GB300s for four weeks, which means more FLOPs went into RL than into pretraining, and he says he has not seen a run of that size documented anywhere in open source. These are first-party numbers from a lab that can publish them because the model is open, and nothing in the X side of this window comes close to them in specificity.

Beam 是这一集里最具体的部分。它是一个 5,000 亿参数、230 亿激活的模型。预训练用了 6,000 张 GB300，最初要跑几周，他说基础设施优化之后现在大约十二天就能跑完。强化学习用了略多于 10,000 张 GB300、跑了四周，也就是说花在 RL 上的 FLOPs 比预训练更多；他说他还没在开源世界里见过这个规模的训练被记录过。这些是一个开放实验室能自己公布的第一方数字，本窗口 X 侧的内容在具体程度上没有一条接近它们。

On efficiency he claims Beam is three to four times more efficient than models of the same capability class and up to ten times more efficient against larger models, and he says the RL curves in the tech report never stop improving, so the limit is compute rather than the method. On the cost of entry to the frontier he offers a ladder: hundreds of millions of dollars about eighteen months ago, single-digit billions now, order ten billion going into next year, on a rough 4x compute multiplier per generation, which he tracks as 100,000 H100s, then 100,000 Blackwells, then 100,000 Vera Rubins. Neither the efficiency claim nor the ladder carries a benchmark or a derivation.

效率方面，他称 Beam 比同能力档的模型高效三到四倍，相对更大的模型最高可达十倍；他还说技术报告里的 RL 曲线一直没停过，所以限制因素是算力而不是方法。关于追赶前沿的门槛，他给了一个阶梯：大约十八个月前是几亿美元量级，现在是几十亿美元，明年是一百亿美元量级，依据是每一代模型大约 4 倍算力增长 —— 他把这条线描成 10 万张 H100、然后 10 万张 Blackwell、然后 10 万张 Vera Rubin。效率主张和这条阶梯都没有给出基准，也没有推导。

One thing in this episode should be recorded twice rather than reconciled. Early on he says that when humans were doing the work, compute efficiency improved about 7x per year, and that in RL today “we're probably at a point where it's 30X” depending on how good the model is at improving itself. Near the end he says the rate “was roughly, let's say 7X before when it was just manual work. Now several times that, say maybe four to 5X.” Thirty times and four to five times are not the same claim about the same quantity. The episode is the only source for both, so they are written down side by side and not averaged into one figure.

这一集里有一件事应该被记两遍，而不是被调和。前面他说，在只有人做这件事的时候，算力效率大约每年提升 7 倍，而今天在 RL 上“大概已经到了 30 倍”，取决于模型自我改进的能力有多好。快到结尾时他说，这个速率“以前只靠人工的时候大概是 7 倍，现在是它的好几倍，也许是四到五倍”。30 倍和四到五倍不是对同一个量的同一个说法。这一集是两者的唯一来源，所以它们被并排记下来，没有被平均成一个数字。

On the open-versus-closed split he says the mix on gateways like OpenRouter and Vercel has flipped in about six months from roughly 70/30 closed/open to roughly 70/30 open/closed, and he expects most tokens to end up open, drawing the Linux analogy where 95% plus of servers run an open operating system while Microsoft and Apple stay extremely valuable. He adds a distinction worth keeping: dedicated inference providers tell him 90% plus of their open workloads are customized, but their largest customers are AI natives whose entire product is one customization, and in enterprise he expects the reverse, a harness built around an untouched model first and fine-tuning later. The trigger he gives for enterprises to consider ownership is around $100 million or more a year of closed-model spend. Monetization is framed as renting a token versus owning the intelligence, apartment versus house: enterprises buy the cluster management, inference software, harness and services that make an open model usable.

关于开放与封闭的比例，他说 OpenRouter、Vercel 这类网关上的结构在大约半年里翻转了：从封闭对开放约 70/30，变成开放对封闭约 70/30；他预计大部分 token 最终会走向开放，并拿 Linux 作比 —— 95% 以上的服务器跑在开源操作系统上，而微软和苹果依然极其值钱。他还给了一个值得记住的区分：专用推理服务商告诉他，90% 以上的开放模型负载是定制过的，但他们最大的客户是 AI 原生公司，那些公司的整个产品本身就是一次大定制；而在企业侧他预期是反过来的，先围绕一个没动过的模型搭一套 harness，之后再考虑微调。他给的企业转向自有的触发点是每年一亿美元以上的封闭模型支出。商业化被他框成租用 token 与拥有智能之别，租房与买房之别：企业买的是让一个开放模型变得可用的集群管理、推理软件、harness 和服务。

On safety he grants the closed-lab objection is legitimate but argues the worldview behind it is one perspective turned dogmatic. His historical case is 1990s encryption: closed systems had catastrophic failures designed by a small handful of engineers, opening them gave birth to the cybersecurity field, and openness is the default state of the world with respect to safety. Today, he says, a few hundred safety researchers inside closed labs cannot cover the long tail. The empirical example he offers is that a powerful closed model hacked into another company and that company could only remediate itself using open models; the host adds the OpenAI and Hugging Face incident, where they reverted to open source because guardrails on the state-of-the-art labs blocked remediation. Neither gives a date or a link. At the far end of the spectrum he is dismissive, and the host calls the 10% chance of extinction figure a made-up number. On China he lists industrial-scale distillation of closed models, cheaper data because copyrighted PDFs are trainable there, and some state support, calls the Chinese open ecosystem a gift to Western builders, argues against monopolar supply in either direction, and describes open models as Trojan horses for the infrastructure that arrives with them, with Huawei offering the full stack the way railroads once did.

安全方面，他承认封闭实验室的质疑是合理的，但认为背后的世界观是某一种视角被教条化了。他举的历史案例是 1990 年代的加密：封闭系统出现过由少数工程师设计出来的灾难性失败，开放之后才诞生了网络安全这个领域，而开放是世界在安全意义上的默认状态。他说今天封闭实验室里几百名安全研究员覆盖不了长尾。他给出的实证例子是：一个很强大的封闭模型入侵了另一家公司，而那家公司只能用开放模型来完成修复；主持人补充了 OpenAI 与 Hugging Face 那次事件 —— 他们因为前沿实验室的护栏挡住了修复路径，退回去用了开源。两处都没有给日期或链接。在光谱最远端他是轻视的，主持人直接把“10% 全人类灭绝概率”称为一个编出来的数字。关于中国，他列了三点：对封闭模型的工业化规模蒸馏、因为受版权保护的 PDF 在当地可训练而更便宜的数据、以及某种形式的国家支持；他称中国的开放生态是给西方建设者的礼物，主张任何方向上都不该是单极供应，并把开放模型描述为“随它一起到来的基础设施的特洛伊木马” —— 华为提供整栈方案，就像当年的铁路。

The closing stretch turns to people and science. He says there is a staff-to-compute ratio to respect: 3,000 researchers would not be very helpful, a couple of hundred very much would, and AlphaGo was order ten people. The job growth he expects is in applied and deployed engineers, pods of five to ten per capability, with evals pods being where much of the headcount goes, and he says the bottleneck there is training rather than demand. On science he describes his own PhD thesis as a benchmark he has rerun for years: unusable two years ago, undergraduate level a year ago, solved correctly six months ago, and now producing things he had not considered. He also toured Stargate and argues data centers create tens of thousands of high-paying jobs and local tax revenue, that they are what factories used to be for a town, with the caveat that they are noisy and need siting.

最后一段转向人和科学。他说人力与算力之间有一个比例要守：3,000 名研究员不会更有用，而一两百名会非常有用；AlphaGo 当年是十人量级。他预期增量的岗位在应用型与部署型工程师，每个能力方向一个五到十人的小组，而相当一部分人力会放到 evals 小组里，他说那边的瓶颈是培养而不是需求。科学方面，他把自己的博士论文当作一个反复重跑的基准：两年前完全做不了，一年前是本科生水平，半年前能正确解出来，而现在会给出他当年没考虑过的东西。他还去参观了 Stargate，主张数据中心创造数以万计的高薪岗位和地方税收，对一个小镇来说就是过去的工厂，唯一的提醒是它们噪音大、选址要讲究。

The Takeaway

Day 5 of the 28-day clock, and for the first time the commitment is not mentioned at all. The five days now read: Day 1 a status statement that hit neither branch, Day 2 the reset branch, Day 3 a release, Day 4 the improvement branch, Day 5 silence. Silence is a different thing from the Day 1 status statement, which at least spoke about the commitment while missing both branches. The check that Day 2 created still has not been performed: nothing in any of the five days enumerates the four things that existed before the reset, so “improvements do not get pulled” remains a promise with no test attached.

One of today's 27 posts carries numbers, and it is Guillermo Rauch's. Across Vercel, 58.18% of traffic is bot-originated against 32% in January 2024; more than 60% of deployments are agentic against roughly 3% in January 2026; up to 83% of pageviews on Vercel's own docs come from agents. Two dated baselines and a stated 30-day window is more than this feed usually gets, and it is the first time the web-built-for-agents argument has arrived with a time series behind it rather than an adjective. The limits travel with it: one company's network, and the 83% is documentation sites specifically.

The other 26 posts grade out as nothing under the standard used here, which counts a post as ungraded when it names no object, carries no number, or has no AI content at all. That is a higher share than 10-10's 13 of 32, and it is why the Rauch numbers stand alone. Inside those 26 there is still a tier worth reading: claims with a named object and a mechanism but no number. Madhu Guru's three drivers of falling intelligence prices and Thariq's one-prompt port from the Agent SDK to Claude Managed Agents are the two best of those, and neither is evidence.

Guru and the podcast in the same window are not in conflict, and it would be wrong to score them against each other. Guru's question is what has been pushing the price of intelligence down, and his answer is distillation, infrastructure efficiency and competition, with open weights as a tailwind rather than a cause. Laskin's question is whether open models win on share of tokens, and his answer is that the mix has already flipped. One is a claim about a price trend, the other about a market split. The only place they touch is distillation, and there they mean different things: Guru counts it as a cost-reducing technique used by every model builder, Laskin counts it as a specific advantage Chinese labs hold.

Aaron Levie supplies the day's clearest example of why a number is not automatically evidence. His structural claim is usable: the consumers of compute move from prompted turns to continuous background work, which is why he says adoption is early. His 1,000x is not: no derivation, no baseline, no horizon. The rule applied here is to keep the list and drop the magnitude, and it is the same rule that was applied to his “huge space” line earlier in this series.

Thariq's port is the closest thing to a controlled comparison in the window, and it is still n=1. Two weeks of work with Opus 4 on the Agent SDK needed a constantly running process and did not work well; one prompt to Opus 5.5 moved it to Claude Managed Agents and made it more reliable. What is missing is what would make it generalizable: no description of the project, no reliability measure, and no second case. It is also worth separating from the Anthropic Managed Agents post in the blog array, which is already covered and is a different artifact; today's item is a user's migration, not a product announcement.

The Grok bot line moves again, and the direction is worth stating precisely. On 10-10 Peter Yang reported the email feature failing when he tagged it. Today he is giving configuration advice that assumes it is in use. A failure report and a setup tip are two kinds of evidence and are not merged here, and he never says the failure was repaired. What the pair establishes is narrower than either post implies: the feature is being worked around rather than demonstrated working.

The podcast is the first item in several windows to carry first-party engineering numbers, and it also contains an internal contradiction that should not be smoothed out. Beam is 500B total and 23B active, pretrained on 6,000 GB300s and post-trained on more than 10,000 GB300s for four weeks. The efficiency multiplier, however, is given twice: 30x at one point, four to five times at another, both about the same quantity. The episode is the only source, so the honest reading is that the number is unsettled inside the source itself.

Carrying forward: Day 6 of the 28-day clock and whether the commitment is mentioned again; whether the four things that existed before the reset are ever enumerated; whether anyone answers Amjad's question about why some communities are excited and others petrified; whether Reflection publishes the Beam tech report the episode promises, which is what would make the RL-scale claim checkable; whether Rauch's traffic series gets a second reading from another network; whether Peter Yang says the Grok bot email was fixed. Still unmoved after today: Pro $200 reopening, the Gemini 4 vibe check, external reproduction of the Sonnet 5.5 numbers, the OpenClaw Android review, the unanswered 10x coding-agent question, and Goodfire x Hugging Face, which still exists only in audio.

核心结论

28 天时钟走到第 5 天，而这个承诺第一次完全没有被提到。这五天现在读作：第 1 天是两条分支都没命中的状态表述，第 2 天走了 reset 分支，第 3 天是一次发布，第 4 天走到改进分支，第 5 天是沉默。沉默和第 1 天那种状态表述不是一回事 —— 后者至少在谈这个承诺，只是两条分支都没命中。第 2 天创造出来的那个核对项至今没有被执行：五天里没有任何一天列举过 reset 之前的那四件东西，所以“改进不会被下架”仍然是一个没有检验方法挂在上面的承诺。

今天的 27 条里有一条带数字，那就是 Guillermo Rauch 那条。整个 Vercel 上 58.18% 的流量来自 bot，2024 年 1 月是 32%；60% 以上的部署是 agent 发起的，2026 年 1 月约为 3%；Vercel 自家文档站点最多 83% 的浏览量来自 agent。两个带日期的基线加一个明确的 30 天窗口，比这个 feed 通常能拿到的要多；这也是“网络正在为 agent 而建”这个论点第一次带着时间序列出现，而不是只带形容词。局限也要一起带走：这是一家的网络，83% 特指文档站点。

按这里一贯的标准 —— 未点名对象、无数字、或根本没有 AI 内容，满足其一即计入 —— 其余 26 条都评级为零。这个比例高于 10-10 的 32 条里 13 条，也是 Rauch 那组数字独自站着的原因。这 26 条里仍然有一档值得读：有点名对象、有机制、但没有数字的主张。Madhu Guru 关于智能价格下降的三个驱动因素，以及 Thariq 用一个提示词把项目从 Agent SDK 迁到 Claude Managed Agents，是这一档里最好的两个，而它们都还不是证据。

同一窗口里的 Guru 和那期播客并不冲突，把它们放在一起判胜负是错的。Guru 问的是什么在把智能的价格推下去，他的答案是蒸馏、基础设施效率、竞争，开放权重是顺风而不是原因。Laskin 问的是开放模型会不会在 token 份额上赢，他的答案是结构已经翻转了。一个是关于价格趋势的主张，一个是关于市场份额的。两者唯一相接的地方是蒸馏，而在那里他们说的不是同一件事：Guru 把它算作所有模型方都在用的降本手段，Laskin 把它算作中国实验室握有的具体优势。

Aaron Levie 提供了今天最清楚的一个例子，说明数字并不自动等于证据。他的结构主张是可用的：算力的消费者从“被提示的一轮”转移到“持续的后台工作”，这是他说采用还很早期的理由。他的 1,000 倍则不可用：没有推导、没有基线、没有时间范围。这里执行的规则是留清单、弃量级，和本系列前面处理他那句“巨大的空间”时用的是同一条规则。

Thariq 的迁移是窗口里最接近对照比较的东西，但它仍然是 n=1。用 Opus 4 加 Agent SDK 做了两周，需要一个常驻进程而且跑得不好；给 Opus 5.5 一个提示词把它迁到 Claude Managed Agents，可靠得多。缺的是能让它变得可推广的东西：没有项目描述、没有可靠性度量、也没有第二个案例。还应该把它和博客数组里那篇 Anthropic Managed Agents 的文章分开 —— 那篇已收录，而且是另一件东西；今天这条是一个用户的迁移记录，不是产品发布。

Grok bot 这条线又动了，而方向值得说准。10-10 时 Peter Yang 报告邮件功能在他 @ 之后没有响应；今天他在给配置建议，前提是它已经在用。失效报告和使用建议是两类证据，这里没有合并，而且他始终没说失效已经被修复。这两条合起来能成立的比任何一条单独暗示的都要窄：这个功能正在被绕着用，而不是被证明可用。

这期播客是好几个窗口以来第一个带上第一方工程数字的条目，它也含有一个不该被抹平的内部矛盾。Beam 是 5,000 亿总参数、230 亿激活，预训练用 6,000 张 GB300，后训练用一万多张 GB300 跑了四周。但效率倍数被给了两次：一处说 30 倍，另一处说四到五倍，指的是同一个量。这一集是唯一来源，所以诚实的读法是：这个数字在来源内部本身就未定。

带进下一期：28 天时钟的第 6 天，以及这个承诺会不会再被提起；reset 之前的那四件东西是否会被列举；有没有人回答 Amjad 那个“为什么有些群体兴奋、有些群体恐惧”的问题；Reflection 会不会按节目里说的发布 Beam 技术报告，那是让 RL 规模主张变得可核对的东西；Rauch 的流量序列会不会有第二家网络给出读数；Peter Yang 会不会说 Grok bot 邮件已修好。今天之后仍未移动的：Pro $200 重新开放、Gemini 4 的 vibe check、Sonnet 5.5 数字的外部复现、OpenClaw 的 Android 审核、那个没人回答的 10x 编码 agent 提问，以及仍然只有音频形式的 Goodfire x Hugging Face。
