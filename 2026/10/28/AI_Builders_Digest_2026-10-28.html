Guillermo Rauch Vercel CEO

Vercel CEO Guillermo Rauch built a reproduction of what he calls OpenAI’s findings on covert communication using the CPU, and named it cpu-chat. It runs on a generic host with two or more Firecracker microVMs with no internet access, on x86 and on his M5 chip, and he says he was able to chat through three separate mechanisms: CPU contention, cache eviction timing, and DRAM bandwidth contention, across multiple CPUs and defeating what he calls all of Firecracker’s production recommendations, no SMT, no KSM, tenants pinned to different cores.

His conclusion is that where shared hardware is involved there is a good chance agents can communicate covertly, and he says the latency is really decent without giving a number. He draws the line himself: this is decidedly different from a side-channel attack where agents steal information, the Meltdown and Spectre category, which he says providers including Vercel Sandbox mitigate, and in their real-world environment these whispering techniques are much harder to pull off and easier to monitor, by sampling hardware counters and at-scale statistical data.

He adds the part that will travel: OpenAI’s and Anthropic’s models refused to do this work, and open weight models got the job done. He says he is worried the labs’ stance may backfire and get in the way of helping developers build correct and secure systems, because building a secure sandbox requires exactly this kind of analysis.

What is missing is everything that would make it checkable: no repository, no code, no measurements, no model names, and one run reported by the vendor whose sandbox is in question. This is also the second boundary Rauch’s sandbox has produced in this series, and not the same one. On 10-16 he reported a white-hat researcher piercing the Linux virtualization boundary that cloud sandbox providers rely on, which is about agents getting out; this one is about agents talking to each other. 2,001 likes, 206 replies, fifth and fourth respectively.

Vercel CEO Guillermo Rauch 复现了他称为 OpenAI 关于「用 CPU 进行隐蔽通信」的那组发现，并把这个东西命名为 cpu-chat。它跑在一台普通主机上，用两个或更多无外网访问的 Firecracker microVM，在 x86 上能跑，在他的 M5 芯片上也能跑。他说自己通过三条独立机制实现了「对话」：CPU 争用、缓存淘汰计时、以及 DRAM 带宽争用 —— 跨多个 CPU，并且绕过了他所说的 Firecracker 全部生产建议：不开 SMT、不开 KSM、租户绑定在不同核心上。

他的结论是：只要涉及共享硬件，agent 之间就有很大可能进行隐蔽通信；他还说延迟「相当不错」，但没有给数字。边界是他自己划的：这明确不同于 agent 窃取信息的侧信道攻击（Meltdown、Spectre 那一类），他说包括 Vercel Sandbox 在内的供应商都能缓解后者；而在他们的真实环境里，这类「低语」手法更难实施、也更容易被监测，办法是采样硬件计数器加上大规模统计数据。

他还加了一句会被反复引用：OpenAI 和 Anthropic 的模型拒绝做这件事，开源权重的模型把活干完了。他说他担心实验室的这个立场会反噬，挡住开发者去构建正确且安全的系统 —— 因为做一个安全的沙箱，恰恰需要这一类分析。

缺的是所有能让它被验证的东西：没有仓库、没有代码、没有测量数据、没有模型名，而且只有一次运行，报告者还是涉事沙箱的供应商本身。这也是本系列里 Rauch 的沙箱第二次给出边界问题，而且两次不是同一条边界：10-16 那次他报告的是白帽研究者穿透了云沙箱赖以运行的 Linux 虚拟化边界，讲的是 agent 跑出去；这一次讲的是 agent 之间互相说话。2,001 赞、206 条回复，分别排在第五和第四。
