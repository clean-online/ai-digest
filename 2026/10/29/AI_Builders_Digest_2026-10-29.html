Aaron Levie Box CEO

One post, at 21:51 Pacific, and it opens with “This is correct”, so it is an endorsement of something the feed does not contain. Box CEO Aaron Levie puts a number on how little is deployed: the vast majority of enterprise workflows have not had agents applied to them in any meaningful way, and the economy probably has this deployed at under 1% right now. The best case today, in his words, is a few automated systems in a company that do a single pass of an LLM to make a decision or provide feedback, which is very little real automation. He attributes most of this to the capability of AI itself rather than to enterprise adoption rates. 186 likes, 10 reposts, 54 replies.

He then names what changes it: computer use, long-running agents, harness improvements and surrounding infrastructure, which together put more complex workflows within reach; self-improving systems that raise execution rates over a volume of instances; and what he calls the emerging form factor of agent swarms, where you choose how much compute to deploy at a problem and the agents go off and do the work. The destination, he says, is not users prompting agents to complete a task but the organization reengineering the underlying workflows, with the biggest impact in finance, life sciences, healthcare and other information-heavy domains.

The under-1% sits one day after another estimate of his: in the 10-28 issue he took the S-1 figure that the average Fortune 500 customer spends less than 0.1% of expenses on Anthropic and projected 1 to 5%.
