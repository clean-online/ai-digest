The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck
Jev: The AI That Makes Decisions, Not Text — and Why It Changes Everything (Diogo Almeida)

The Takeaway — A model that answers in probabilities instead of prose is a different product, not a smaller one, and the reason it is cheap is the same reason it is useful.

Diogo Almeida runs TypeSafe, the company behind Jev, and was at OpenAI before that; the 2026-10-02 issue of this series recorded a Decisions API credit going to a Diogo, formerly at OpenAI, for having pushed this class of idea for a long time. Jev came out of stealth about three weeks before this recording, and the host puts the launch video at 40 million views, alongside what he says he has heard about a million users a day, a trillion tokens a day and a third of the Fortune 500, plus a Series A of $870M at a $7.5B valuation. Almeida waves the numbers off: “the leading indicator of it all is builders being inspired to do new things”, and the metric he actually cares about is intelligence per dollar.

The mechanism is the part worth reading. Where a language model writes you an answer, Jev returns scores: is this email urgent, should it escalate, which department. His example is an email assistant, which he says language models still have not solved, because you end up “programming in natural language this giant system prompt” and the error rate climbs with the complexity. Replace that with individual classifiers and the system becomes legible: you can measure each one, none of them get forgotten, and a mistake becomes an eval. He wants probabilities rather than yes or no because the threshold belongs to the developer, and Disney’s threshold will not be the same as a role-play product’s.

He is unbothered by being called a classifier: “How does Facebook work? Classifiers. How does TikTok work? Classifiers. How does Google work? Classifiers.” His one figure for reliability is the jaggedness: models are “95% Einstein, 5% Mr. Bean”, and “if it doesn’t tell you when it’s the Mr. Bean, then how are you gonna automate anything?” On the copycats that arrived within three weeks, from Cloudflare, Databricks and Microsoft: “My guess is that they are dumb and non-robust, but I want to measure this before I say that to anyone else.” His stated fear is that a bad copy convinces people the whole category does not work. He also says he did not pretrain: “pre training is a giant dumpster fire of wasting resources.”

Two things to keep separate. The episode is dated 2026-10-15 and reached this feed ten days later, so none of it is new in calendar terms. And the feed gives a channel URL rather than a link to this episode, which is why the link below goes to the channel.

核心结论 —— 一个用概率而不是用文字回答的模型，是另一种产品，不是更小的一号；而它便宜的原因，正是它有用的原因。

Diogo Almeida 经营 TypeSafe，Jev 背后的公司，此前在 OpenAI；本系列 2026-10-02 那期记过一句，说 Decisions API 这个方向的功劳要给一位「曾在 OpenAI 的 Diogo」，他推这类想法推了很久。Jev 在这次录制前三周左右走出隐身状态，主持人给出的数字是：发布视频 4,000 万次观看，还有他听说的每天约一百万用户、每天一万亿 token、以及财富 500 强里的三分之一，外加 8.7 亿美元 A 轮、75 亿美元估值。Almeida 把这些数字挥开：「真正的先行指标，是做东西的人被激发去做新东西」，而他真正在意的度量是「每一美元买到多少智能」。

值得读的是机制。语言模型给你写出一段回答，Jev 给你返回分数：这封邮件急不急、要不要升级、转给哪个部门。他举的例子是邮件助理，他说语言模型到今天也没解决它，因为最后你会变成「用自然语言去写这一大段 system prompt」，而错误率随着复杂度一起往上走。把它换成一个个单独的分类器，系统就变得可读：每一个都能测量，没有一个会被遗漏，出一次错就变成一条评测。他要概率而不是 yes/no，是因为阈值该由开发者来定，而迪士尼的阈值不会和一个角色扮演产品一样。

被叫做分类器他并不介意：「Facebook 靠什么运转？分类器。TikTok 靠什么运转？分类器。Google 靠什么运转？分类器。」他给可靠性下的唯一一个说法是那种参差感：模型是「95% 的爱因斯坦，5% 的憨豆先生」，而「如果它不告诉你它什么时候是憨豆先生，你要怎么把任何东西自动化？」对三周之内出现的跟随者，Cloudflare、Databricks 和微软：「我猜它们很笨而且不稳定，但我想先量过再对别人这么说。」他明说的担心是：一个糟糕的仿品会让人以为这一整类模型都不行。他还说自己没有做预训练：「预训练是一场巨大的资源浪费。」

The Takeaway

The clock resets again, and this is now the clearest pattern the Day N series has produced. Day 10, Day 15 and Day 20 are all resets; Day 16 is the only entry that named a change and put a number on it, and it did so without mentioning the reset that came the day before. Day 20 adds two lines with no object: “It is all propagated” does not say what, and “By midnight PST” is a deadline rather than a delivery. Whether anything arrived by that midnight is the first thing the next issue can check.

The podcast slot produces something for the first time since the 10-19 issue, and it is the largest single piece of content in this issue. Jev has been in this series since 09-18, when Rauch reported an 18x speed figure and said it was coming to Vercel AI Gateway, and it has been mentioned on 09-20, 09-24, 10-02 and 10-03. Until now it has only ever been somebody else’s one-line claim. This is the first time the series has the person behind it explaining the mechanism, and the mechanism is specific: probabilities instead of text, classifiers instead of a system prompt, and legibility as the reason.

Two builders write about people today, three and a half hours apart, and neither mentions the other. Ryo Lu’s essay asks what is lost when the difficulty goes away and answers with “I want people to struggle with”. Aditya Agarwal says being fully bought in on AI made him treasure time with family and friends. Two framings, one direction, no shared measurement, and no post in this window refers to another, the same shape as the four builders who pointed one way in the 10-22 issue.

Only two numbers exist in this window and neither is a measurement of anything shipped. Levie’s 1,000X is a forecast about token volume through cyber agents with no baseline and no date. Rauch’s 900, from ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English, is a reference point for a question he does not answer, how small a working vocabulary for models could be. Everything else is names without descriptions, from Day 20’s unnamed rollout to Alex Albert’s Claude Managed Agents and Duet.

One thing this issue cannot do is rank by attention. Every one of the 23 posts came back with likes, reposts, replies and quotes null, so the ordering here is by substance, not by reach. That is a change from the 10-21 and 10-22 issues, where popularity and content could be set against each other.

核心结论

时钟又 reset 了一次，而这已经是 Day N 这个系列里最清楚的一个模式。Day 10、Day 15、Day 20 三次都是 reset；Day 16 是唯一一次既点名变更又给出数字的，而它完全没有提前一天发生的那次 reset。Day 20 又加了两条没有宾语的话：「It is all propagated」没有说传播了什么，「By midnight PST」是一个截止时间而不是一次交付。那个午夜之前到底有没有东西落地，是下一期第一个可以查的。

播客这一格自 10-19 那期以来第一次有产出，而且它是这一期里最大的一块内容。Jev 从 09-18 起就在本系列里了，那天 Rauch 报了一个 18 倍的速度数字，说它会进 Vercel AI Gateway；之后 09-20、09-24、10-02、10-03 都提过。在那之前，它一直只是别人嘴里的一句话。这是本系列第一次拿到它背后的人亲口讲机制，而机制是具体的：用概率代替文字，用分类器代替 system prompt，理由是可读性。

今天有两位写到人，相隔三个半小时，谁也没提谁。Ryo Lu 那篇问的是困难被拿走之后失去了什么，回答是「我想要可以一起扛的人」。Aditya Agarwal 说，被 AI 彻底说服反而让他更珍惜和家人朋友的时间。两种说法、一个方向、没有共同的测量，这个窗口里也没有任何一条推文提到另一条——和 10-22 那期四位 builder 指向同一方向的形状是一样的。

这个窗口里只有两个数字，而且都不是对已交付之物的测量。Levie 的 1,000 倍是一个关于流经安全 agent 的 token 量的预测，没有基线也没有日期。Rauch 的 900 来自 ASD-STE100 简化技术英语，是他自己也没回答的那个问题的参照点：模型可用的词汇量到底能小到什么程度。其余全是只有名字、没有说明的东西，从 Day 20 那次没有点名的上线，到 Alex Albert 的 Claude Managed Agents 和 Duet。

这一期有一件事做不到：按热度排序。23 条推文的赞、转、回复、引用全部返回为空，所以这里的排序是按内容，不是按触达。这和 10-21、10-22 两期不同，那两期可以把热度和内容放在一起来看。
