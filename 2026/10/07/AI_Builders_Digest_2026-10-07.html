一条，四句，而它是一个定义，不是一个观察。「团队最常犯的错误，是在 agent 造好之后把 evals 当成一道额外的 QA 环节。AI 产品根本不同。你的 evals 就是你的产品说明书。」这个动作是位置上的：evals 从流程的末尾挪到了开头，从「检查产品的东西」变成「定义产品的东西」。他没有点名任何框架、公司或数字，也没有交代那些已经在没有它的情况下交付了的团队该怎么办。

一条，它是昨天的论证没有的那件实物。gdp-ts 把「Ghosts of Departed Proofs」带进了 TypeScript：一个库、一个 linter、一个 AI skill，在这个约定下，敏感函数要求调用方拿出「已经做过授权检查」的证明，由类型检查器在编译期验证这些证明，「防止你的团队和 agent 把灾难性的安全（以及其他类型的）bug 发上线」。他建模的例子是一条真实的 Vercel 约束，不是一个玩具：修改一个 Project 的密码，需要一份「具备某个角色 + 某项权益」的证明。

The half worth keeping is his account of the timing. The pattern is old and comes from Haskell, and he credits Matt Noonan and Ollie Charles for the research. What kept it niche, he says, was two things: human code review, and cognitive and syntactic overhead. His claim is that both have inverted. “Agents are writing more code than we can review, and they *thrive* in tight loops with hard constraints that would frustrate us.” That is the same thesis he stated yesterday about the completed Go-to-Rust migration, when he said the criterion had moved underneath it: what is best for humans is no longer necessarily best for business.

值得留下的那半边，是他讲的时机。这个模式很老，来自 Haskell，他致谢了 Matt Noonan 和 Ollie Charles 的研究。他说，让它一直小众的是两件事：人工代码评审，以及认知与语法上的开销。他的主张是这两件都翻转了：「agent 写的代码已经超过我们能评审的量，而且它们在硬约束的紧循环里如鱼得水 —— 那种循环会让我们烦躁。」这正是他昨天讲那次已完成的 Go→Rust 迁移时的同一个论点，当时他说评判标准在它脚下移动了：对「人」最好的，不再必然是对「生意」最好的。

What changed between the two posts is the shape of the evidence, not the amount of it. Yesterday was a retrospective cost admission about finished work, called controversial internally, carrying no number. Today there is a library with a named mechanism, and it can be installed and argued with. Still unmeasured in both: no benchmark, no defect rate, no adoption figure. One continuity worth noting — he reaches for the same evidence list he used yesterday, the rise of Rust, the borrow checker, the code aesthetics debate, which means the two posts are one argument told twice rather than two arguments.

两帖之间变的是证据的形状，不是证据的量。昨天是一次针对已完成工作的事后成本承认，被他称为内部有争议，没有数字。今天有一个带名字机制的库，可以装上来争论。两者都仍然没有被测量：没有基准、没有缺陷率、没有采用数。有一点连贯性值得记 —— 他用的是昨天那份同样的证据清单：Rust 的兴起、借用检查器、代码审美的争论，这意味着两帖是同一个论点讲了两次，而不是两个论点。

Evals moved to the front of the process today, twice, from opposite sides of the market. Rauch closed the gap he opened yesterday. On 10-06 he argued that the criterion for choosing a language had moved because agents now write more code than humans can review, and he said it as a contested cost admission about a migration already finished, with no numbers. Today he ships gdp-ts, which applies exactly that thesis in TypeScript: sensitive functions demand compile-time proofs that an authorization check happened. His account of the timing is the part worth keeping — the pattern is old and comes from Haskell, and what kept it niche was human code review plus cognitive overhead, both of which he says have now inverted. Still no measurement: no benchmark, no defect rate, no adoption. What changed is the shape of the evidence, from a retrospective judgement to something that can be installed and argued with.
