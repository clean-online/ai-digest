Google’s AI Infrastructure Chief, Amin Vahdat, on the Physics & Economics of Frontier AI

Google’s AI infrastructure chief Amin Vahdat is running one of the largest capital buildouts in the industry — the host puts Google’s CapEx above $200 billion this year — and he refuses to be measured in FLOPS. FLOPS, he says, is a theoretical maximum under chosen conditions; what his team is held to is goodput, the performance a real workload actually delivers once failures, restarts and recovery are counted. It is a Google term, he adds, that others are starting to pick up. The reason it matters is scale: at 100,000 accelerators working synchronously at microsecond-to-millisecond granularity, something fails “multiple times a day and perhaps… multiple times an hour”, and one failure can stall the entire job while everyone waits for a checkpoint to be restored. Asked for the most common failure reason, he says there isn’t one: “If there were a common failure reason, we’d have figured it out and fixed it. It is a long tail of constant discovery.”

Google AI 基础设施负责人 Amin Vahdat 正在操盘业内规模最大的一次资本开支 —— 主持人给出的数字是 Google 今年 CapEx 超过 2,000 亿美元 —— 而他拒绝用 FLOPS 被衡量。他说 FLOPS 是选定条件下的理论上限；他的团队被考核的是 goodput，即一个真实工作负载在把故障、重启与恢复都算进去之后，实际交付出来的性能。他补了一句，这是 Google 的术语，业内开始有人跟着用。它之所以重要是因为规模：在十万块加速器以微秒到毫秒的粒度同步工作时，“一天会坏很多次，取决于配置，可能一小时就坏很多次”，而一次故障就能让整个任务停下来，所有人等着从某个检查点恢复。被问到最常见的故障原因时，他说没有那种东西：“如果有常见原因，我们早就查出来修好了。这是一条不断有新发现的长尾。”

The second move he makes is to shift credit for progress away from silicon. Google’s target is to double effective serving capacity, measured as token generation capability, roughly every six months, and his claim is that “as much or more of that is going to come from software as it is from hardware”, through dozens or hundreds of individual optimizations landing again and again. Asked how the gains have actually split, he says he does not have the exact breakdown, but that in his experience most of the improvement in intelligence per watt has come from the model side, a fair amount from software systems, and that hardware supplies a multiplier that “lifts everyone else up year over year” at 2x or more. The breakdown is his recollection rather than a measurement, and it comes from the person accountable for the hardware.

他的第二个动作，是把进步的功劳从芯片身上挪走。Google 的目标是让有效服务容量（以 token 生成能力衡量）大约每六个月翻一倍，而他的说法是“这里面来自软件的至少和来自硬件的一样多，甚至更多”，靠的是几十上百个一次次落地的单独优化。被问到实际拆分比例时，他说没有精确的数字，但按他的经验，每瓦智能的提升大部分来自模型侧，软件系统贡献相当一部分，硬件提供的是一个“逐年把所有其他人抬起来”的乘数，2 倍甚至更多。这个拆分是他的回忆而不是测量，而且出自对硬件负责的那个人。

The TPU program is his case for specialisation, told as a bet against the grain. It started in 2013, when the conventional wisdom was that you do not build custom silicon for a single workload — he calls it “the bitter lesson of chips”, that specialisation never wins — and he says quite a few people inside Google were unsure it would work. The first chip was inference only, the second added training, and then transformers arrived and, in his words, “completely shifted the TPU program”. This year the program split into two chips, 8I for inference and 8T for training, and the calculus he describes is about projected market size rather than speed: a serving chip that is 2x faster does not justify itself if serving is going to be 2% or 5% of the market. Both chips can still run the other’s workload, which is what keeps them fungible across a six-year hardware lifetime.

TPU 项目是他为专用化辩护的案例，而且是被他讲成一次逆向下注的赌博。它起步于 2013 年，当时的共识是不该为单一工作负载做定制芯片 —— 他称之为“芯片版的 bitter lesson”，即专用化永远赢不了 —— 而且他说 Google 内部也有不少人不确定它能不能成。第一块芯片只做推理，第二块加上了训练，然后 transformer 出现了，用他的话说“彻底改变了 TPU 项目”。今年这个项目拆成了两块芯片，8I 做推理、8T 做训练，而他描述的算盘打的是预估市场规模而不是速度：一块在服务上快 2 倍的芯片，如果服务只占市场的 2% 或 5%，就不值得做。两块芯片都还能跑对方的工作负载，这正是它们在六年硬件寿命里保持可互换的原因。

Co-design is where he locates the advantage, and he is specific about the mechanism: DeepMind researchers and chip engineers get into the same room for days or weeks, and when a model optimization needs hardware support they will change a chip definition in flight or delay a tape out by a week or two. Across company boundaries he calls that “somewhere between hard and impossible”. Hardware is planned two to five years ahead across five or six pipeline stages, so the trade is explicit — researchers have learned not to bring a 0.5% tweak to a chip that is about to tape out. Asked whether the architecture has been rewritten as models changed, he says that at a medium level of detail it has not really changed since TPU v1: large matrix multiply units, a sparse core, and remote load and store over the ICI network have carried generations of models. They are, he says, “using Gemini to design hardware for future Geminis”.

协同设计是他定位优势的地方，而且他把机制讲得很具体：DeepMind 的研究员和芯片工程师会关在同一间屋子里待上几天到几周，当某个模型优化需要硬件支持时，他们会改动正在执行中的芯片定义，或者把一次 tape out 推迟一两周。跨公司边界做这件事，他用“介于困难和不可能之间”来形容。硬件是提前两到五年、分五六个流水线阶段来规划的，所以这个交换条件是明确的 —— 研究员们已经学会不要把 0.5% 的微调拿去打扰一块马上要 tape out 的芯片。被问到架构有没有随着模型变化被重写，他说在中等细节层面上，从 TPU v1 以来它其实没怎么变：大型矩阵乘单元、一个 sparse core、以及通过 ICI 网络做远程读写，这几样撑住了好几代模型。他说他们正在“用 Gemini 为未来的 Gemini 设计硬件”。

Two forward-looking claims carry the most uncertainty. Long-horizon agents change the shape of the load: with no human in the loop to rate-limit, interaction time drops from seconds to milliseconds, and the parsing and orchestration land on CPUs, so demand for CPU, networking and storage rises alongside accelerated compute — which fights the specialisation he has just argued for, because a CPU rack beside a TPU rack breaks the density and power uniformity the building was designed around. Power he calls “the single most fundamental constraint that we face”, and his preference is grid connection over generating his own, on the argument of statistical multiplexing.

两个朝前看的说法带着最多不确定性。长程 agent 改变了负载的形状：没有人在这个循环里做限速，交互时间从几秒掉到几毫秒，而解析与编排落在 CPU 上，于是 CPU、网络和存储的需求随着加速算力一起往上涨 —— 这恰好和他刚才主张的专用化打架，因为把 CPU 机架放在 TPU 机架旁边，会破坏整栋楼是按之设计的密度与配电均一性。电力被他称为“我们面对的最根本的那一个约束”，而他偏向并网而不是自己发电，理由是统计复用。

He also puts a number on hardware lifetime that cuts against the prevailing debate: seven- and eight-year-old TPUs are still at 100% utilization, even though the depreciation lifetime is about six years and newer generations are more power efficient. Replacement happens by pod rather than by chip — a pod might be 9,600 chips across roughly 150 racks — and the new pod’s footprint often does not fit the vacancy the old one leaves, so retrofitting is “a bit of an art”. Asked what the frontier supercomputer looks like in ten years, he first declines: “the cone of uncertainty there is way, way too wide”. Then he offers it anyway — racks manufactured centrally, deeply integrated, possibly multiple megawatts each, wheeled in and plugged into water, power and fiber. On orbital data centres, which Google is pursuing as a moonshot, his arithmetic is 1.4x solar irradiance from having no atmospheric attenuation and 90% to 100% sunlight coverage in sun-synchronous orbit against roughly 28% to 35% on land; he immediately lists what gets harder, since cooling in space is harder rather than easier and repair is harder, and his summary is “no fundamental showstoppers”.

他还给了一个与当下争论方向相反的硬件寿命数字：七年前和八年前的 TPU 仍然跑在 100% 利用率上，尽管折旧年限大约是六年，而新的一代更省电。更换是按 pod 而不是按芯片进行的 —— 一个 pod 可能是 9,600 块芯片、约 150 个机架 —— 而新 pod 的占地常常对不上旧 pod 留出来的空位，所以改造“有点像一门手艺”。被问到十年后最前沿的超算长什么样，他先拒绝了：“那里的不确定性锥形太宽太宽了”。然后他还是给了 —— 机架集中制造、深度集成、每个可能好几兆瓦，推进来接上水、电和光纤就行。至于太空数据中心（Google 正把它当 moonshot 在推进），他给的算术是：没有大气衰减带来 1.4 倍的太阳辐照，加上太阳同步轨道上 90% 到 100% 的光照覆盖率，对比陆地上的约 28% 到 35%；他紧接着列出变难的部分 —— 太空里的散热是更难而不是更容易，维修也更难 —— 而他的总结是“没有根本性的拦路石”。

28 天的时钟走到第 4 天，第一次落在「改进」这一支。这四天现在读起来是：第 1 天给出的是两条分支都没命中的状态表述，第 2 天走了 reset 分支，第 3 天走了「发布」这条第三条路，第 4 天说的是“我们把 steering 改成了即时生效”。这正是承诺写下来的那一支，用了四天才走到。没变的是测量的缺席：即时、快得多、实时、不白花力气，全是形容词；而第 3 天第一次被追问的票数，已经连续第二天没有出现。
