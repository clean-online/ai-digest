Aaron Levie CEO · Box

One post, the longest piece of argument in the feed, and it is a thesis about where the money goes rather than a news item: the opportunity is in being “the deployment layer for AI into the economy,” because “the amount of work it takes to change out workflows in enterprises tends to be far greater than anyone realizes or would prefer.” He enumerates that work — moving legacy systems to the cloud, updating data organization and access, connecting software to agents in new ways, reengineering workflows for agents, figuring out HITL, generating and maintaining evals, and keeping the whole thing current as models change — and adds that “the full list may even be longer.” The distinction he is drawing is between deploying a tool and delivering output: with software you implemented a well understood category and then stepped back, whereas with agents “you’re deploying work output in a process.” His forecast is a services market split by industry, by company size and by problem inside companies, with traditional SIs adapting unevenly and new firms founded inside the window. It is a vendor describing a market it sells into, and it carries no numbers, no customers and no named examples.

一条，是 feed 里最长的一段论证，讲的是钱会流向哪里，不是一条新闻：机会在于做“AI 进入实体经济的部署层”，因为“在企业里换掉一套工作流要花的工夫，远比任何人以为的、或者希望的多”。他把这些工作逐项列出来：把遗留系统迁上云、更新数据的组织与访问方式、把软件以新的方式接到 agent 上、为 agent 重新设计工作流、把 HITL 想清楚、生成并维护评测，以及随着新模型发布持续更新整个系统——并且补了一句“这份清单可能还更长”。他真正在划的界线，是部署工具和交付产出之间的区别：软件时代你实施的是一个已被充分理解的品类，做完就退后；而有了 agent，“你交付的是流程中的工作产出”。他预测会出现一个按行业、按公司规模、按企业内部具体问题切分的服务市场，传统 SI 会现代化但适应程度参差不齐，窗口期内也会有新公司成立。这是厂商对自己所处市场的自我论述，没有数字、没有客户、没有点名的例子。

How Sam Altman Uses Dots to Take Back His Time

Dan Shipper opens by renaming AI & I to the Every Podcast, and this first episode is recorded at DevDay. Asked what riding the rising capability of coding models has taught him, Altman answers with demand rather than displacement: “people just want so much more stuff,” developers “have never been able to do more, but there’s never been a higher bar either,” and what surprised him on the upside was “how much latent demand existed in the world” and how much people “have remained at the center of everything.” He does concede one direction: there will be areas “where there’s actually not much creativity required and we don’t care about the person doing it,” and those will be done by AI. His reason for staying human-centered is blunter than the usual framing — “we just, we don’t care that much about what the machines do” — and his illustration is the Navier-Stokes episode, where 10,000 agents worked four days in coordination and almost none of the public reaction was about the problem being solved.

Dan Shipper 在开场把 AI & I 改名为 Every Podcast，而这第一期是在 DevDay 现场录的。被问到模型编码能力一路走高教会了他什么，Altman 的回答落在需求而不是替代上：“人们就是想要多得多的东西”，开发者“从未有过这么强的能力，但也从未有过这么高的门槛”，而真正让他意外的是向上的那一侧——“世界上潜藏着多么大的需求”，以及人在整个过程中“始终处在中心”。他也确实让步了一个方向：会有一些领域“其实不需要多少创造力，我们也不在乎是谁在做”，那些会由 AI 完成。而他给出的“为什么仍然以人为中心”的理由，比常见的说法更直白——“我们其实并不那么在乎机器做了什么”——他的例子是 Navier-Stokes 那次：一万个 agent 协同工作了四天，而公众反应几乎没有一条是关于问题本身被解出来的。

Dots is the product the episode is named for, and the account is entirely personal. It is the first thing, he says, that has given him back his mornings: early morning used to be his deepest work window, running a large company meant checking his phone immediately and polluting that window, and now he trusts his dot to judge what needs him and handle the rest. Two incidents come from the day itself. Five minutes before the keynote demo, his dot messaged that it could see he was in the DevDay keynote, knew what the demo was, and that the setup would probably break it — would he like it to try to fix it. It could not fix it yet, and Altman floats that a next version might go fix it in prod. Then, before this recording, in a day of back-to-back commitments, the dot used his only break to surface one thing he had to pay attention to, which he says he would never have found. There is also a failure that turned into a success: he spent 10 to 20 minutes failing to find something in Slack with Codex, concluded he might have dreamed it, and the next day the dot told him it had kept thinking overnight and found it — it had been a screenshot, and the dot had gone through screenshots one by one. His dot is called Dottie, Shipper’s is Boo, both say it has become their default way to use chat, and both flag it as rough and buggy.

Dots 是这期标题里的产品，而关于它的全部描述都是个人体验。他说这是第一件把他的早晨还给了他的东西：清晨原本是他最能做深度工作的时间段，管理一家大公司意味着必须立刻看手机，把这个窗口污染掉，而现在他信任他的 dot 去判断什么需要他、其余自己处理掉。两个案例都发生在当天。keynote demo 开始前五分钟，他的 dot 发消息说它看到他在 DevDay keynote 现场、知道 demo 是什么、并且这套 setup 大概会让 demo 挂掉——问要不要它试着修。它当时还修不了，Altman 顺口说下一代也许能直接去生产环境修。然后，在录这一期之前，在一整天的连轴安排里，dot 利用他仅有的一段空隙提示了一件他必须注意的事，他说这件事他自己永远找不到。还有一个先失败后成功的例子：他用 Codex 在 Slack 里找一样东西找了 10 到 20 分钟没找到，甚至怀疑自己是不是记错了，结果第二天 dot 告诉他它想了一夜并找到了——那东西其实是一张截图，dot 是一张一张翻截图找到的。他的 dot 叫 Dottie，Shipper 的叫 Boo；两人都说它已经成了自己用聊天的默认方式，也都说它现在还很粗糙、有 bug。

The Takeaway

The second promise closes today, and it closes on the artifact rather than on a report of it. Yesterday’s issue flagged that Dan Shipper said a Sam Altman podcast would drop tomorrow, said from inside a window ending 23:45 Pacific on 09-29, so tomorrow meant 09-30 Pacific. This feed carries it: How Sam Altman Uses Dots to Take Back His Time on AI & I by Every, publishedAt 2026-09-30T20:05:56Z, which is 13:05 Pacific on 09-30. Verdict: delivered. Note what did not deliver it: Shipper’s own three posts in this feed are two vibe-check lines and a pointer to Every, none of which mentions the episode.

DevDay gets a number, and it comes from the person who gave the keynote rather than from the people watching it. Shipper counts 22 new things, against about nine last year and four or five the year before, and Altman’s additions are that they cut items to fit 50 minutes and “just wait for next year.” The count is the host’s, uncorrected on tape, and it is not an official list — which matters because the items actually named across the last three issues still come to about nine: Dots, 6.1 Sol, Ultrafast, CUA, Decisions API, MCP servers in ChatGPT, Space, native apps, subscription portability. Nobody has published the list of 22, so the number is currently a claim about volume with no inventory behind it.

The two things Altman says are not solved are worth more than the launches, because they are the only statements in this feed that set a limit. On the App Store: “we clearly have not yet figured out the App Store equivalent.” On speed: Ultrafast is available now if you pay, “8X at a great price in the not distant future,” and 100X after that. Both are said from the stage by the person shipping them, which is a different grade of claim than a launch announcement, and the second is a dated-ish roadmap rather than a result.

Dots is the only product here with an actual use account, and it comes with its own failure case, which is what makes it usable. Altman describes his mornings back, then gives the keynote incident: the dot detected that the demo setup would break the demo, proposed fixing it, and could not. That is the only place in this feed where a limit is stated rather than implied — detection and proposal yes, action no — and his own framing is that a later version might fix it in prod. The Slack screenshot story is the mirror image: he gave up after 10 to 20 minutes with Codex, and the dot found it overnight by going through images one by one. All of it is n=1, all self-reported, and both he and Shipper call the product rough and buggy in the same breath.

Astra is the yardstick for the third day running, and today it is used differently again. On 09-30 Shipper used it as a taste comparison, on 10-01 Altman used it as a price anchor, and today he uses it as a cause: the most important ingredient behind Dots was Astra, because they needed a model past the intelligence bar. Same rival, three different jobs, and not one benchmark in any of the three. That pattern is worth tracking as rhetoric rather than as measurement.

Gemini 4 Argon lands inside the same window, and the judgement around it splits along the same line Levie argues at length. Woodward announces it in three words and nothing else; Peter Yang says the model is competitive but the coding harness (Antigravity) and the personal agent (Spark) are not; Shipper thought it had already shipped and is asking Pichai for a vibe check, with no stated criteria. Levie’s post the same day argues the work is in the deployment layer, which is a vendor arguing his own book — but three independent people pointing at the layer around the model rather than at the model is a stronger signal than any one of them alone. None of the three Gemini claims is measurable here.

核心结论

第二个承诺今天收口，而且是靠实物收口的，不是靠关于它的转述。上一期标记了 Dan Shipper 说和 Sam Altman 的播客明天放出；那句话出自一个止于太平洋时间 09-29 23:45 的窗口，所以“明天”指 09-30。本期 feed 里就有：How Sam Altman Uses Dots to Take Back His Time，AI & I by Every，publishedAt 2026-09-30T20:05:56Z，即太平洋时间 09-30 13:05。判定：兑现。值得注意的是兑现它的并不是他本人那三条帖子——本期 Shipper 的三条是两行 vibe check 加一个指向 Every 的链接，没有一条提到这期节目。

Altman 说“还没解决”的那两件事比发布物更值钱，因为它们是今天 feed 里唯一划出边界的表述。关于 App Store：“我们显然还没找到 App Store 的等价物。” 关于速度：Ultrafast 现在付钱就能用，“在不远的将来会有一个好价格下的 8 倍”，再之后是 100 倍。两句话都是做产品的人站在台上自己说的，和发布公告不是一个等级的主张；第二句更像一条带模糊时间点的路线图，而不是一个结果。
