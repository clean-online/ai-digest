The Takeaway

Day 7 of the 28-day clock, and it is the first day that ships a number. Default context for GPT-6.1 Sol through Codex and Work moves from 272k to 500k, written as about an 80% increase, with 1M left as the override ceiling. The improvement branch was hit on Day 4 with adjectives alone; today it is hit with a before and an after. What did not arrive with it is the part that would make it checkable: no compaction rate before or after, no task set, no sample, and no description of the “carefully tested” beyond the phrase. One small arithmetic note, since the number is the point: 500k against 272k is a factor of 1.84, so the true increase is nearer 84% than the 80 he writes. A rounding, not an error, and it does not change the conclusion — this is the most concrete output the clock has produced in seven days.

The Pro line moved twice on 10-12 and still has no count on it. Yesterday's poll stopped at “Pro users, would you rather have,” and the issue recorded that its subject could not be read from the feed. Today the subject is readable: 2X usage across Pro 100, 200 and 500, at the cost of bringing back 5-hour limits, in order to smooth the demand curve. That names the $200 tier in a concrete trade-off, which none of the nine silent windows ever did. What is still missing is the vote itself: he reports that the result confused him and that he expected it to sway much more towards 2X, so the only direction available is “less than he expected,” with no scale to place it on and no stated next step. Yesterday's check now has a sharper form: whether 5-hour limits actually come back.

The funnel is today's centre of gravity, and it is one source rather than three. Karpathy's four-tier distribution — 6B untouched, 1–2B casual, 20M professional, 5,000 internal — is napkin math with no method behind any tier, and both Levie and Garry Tan are quoting it. So the apparent consensus about how capability is distributed is one post and two reactions. What the two reactions add is real but different in kind: Levie adds the change management that has to happen before swarms reach anyone outside software, and Tan adds an investment claim about the price coming down. The number to keep is 5,000, together with Karpathy's own observation that at that tier human review is already falling behind.

Falling behind is the theme the rest of the feed is answering, and it answers three different objects. Thariq is talking about the model: do not settle for an opaque superintelligence, because communication is itself a signal of intelligence. Micky, via Peter Yang, is talking about acceptance: evidence-driven testing before and after, greploop, the agent presenting its own evidence, manual review as the last step. Garry Tan is talking about the code: replace LLM calls with deterministic code. Same neighbourhood, three objects, no citations between them, so by the 10-11 rule they sit side by side rather than confirming one another. None of the three carries a number.

Decompilation shows up for the fourth day running and still has no instance attached to it. Today Karpathy lists “decompiling apps from binaries” among the things the 20M tier now watches happen first-hand. On 10-08 Amjad made the claim, on 10-09 Peter Yang supplied the economic consequence, and neither produced a case. A capability appearing inside a list of capabilities is not an example of it being done, and four appearances without one is the finding.

Turck's “It will work out just fine” is the day's weakest claim per word. The historical chain it rests on is accurate as a chain — punch cards, Lotus Notes, Flash, DevOps — and the analogy is doing all the work that data is not. It is worth putting next to Levie, who is describing the same transition from the other end and whose version includes the parts that have to be rebuilt first. Both are unfunded by numbers; only one of them says what has to happen.

核心结论

28 天时钟走到 Day 7，这是它第一次带着数字出现。GPT-6.1 Sol 在 Codex 与 Work 中的默认上下文从 272k 提到 500k，写作约 80% 提升，1M 仍作为用户可覆盖的上限。改进这一支在 Day 4 被命中过，但那次只有形容词；今天这次有前有后。没跟着一起来的是让它可被核对的那部分：没有压缩率的前后对比，没有任务集，没有样本，“仔细测试过”也只是一句话。补一个小算术，因为数字正是重点：500k 相对 272k 是 1.84 倍，实际更接近 +84% 而不是他写的 80%。这是舍入，不是错误，也不改变结论 —— 它仍然是这个时钟七天里最具体的一次产出。

Pro 这条线在 10-12 动了两次，但票数仍然没有。昨天那个投票停在“Pro users, would you rather have”，当时记录的是「投票问的是什么在 feed 里读不出来」。今天对象读出来了：Pro 100/200/500 三档都给 2X 额度，代价是恢复 5 小时限制，目的是拉平需求曲线。这把 $200 这一档放进了一个具体的取舍里，而此前沉默的九个窗口一次都没有这样点过名。仍然缺的是票数本身：他只说自己被结果搞糊涂了、本以为会更大幅度偏向 2X，所以能确定的方向只有「比他预期的低」，没有刻度可放，他也没有说下一步。昨天那个核对项现在有了更具体的形式：5 小时限制到底会不会回来。

漏斗是今天的重心，而它是一个信源，不是三个。Karpathy 那四层分布 —— 60 亿没接触过、10–20 亿轻度使用、2,000 万专业使用、5,000 人有内部访问 —— 是餐巾纸估算，每一层背后都没有方法；Levie 和 Garry Tan 都是在引用它。所以那个关于能力如何分布的「一致看法」，是一条推文加两条反应。两条反应补的东西是真的，但性质不同：Levie 补的是集群要走到软件之外的人手里之前必须先做完的变更管理，Tan 补的是关于价格会降下来的投资判断。值得记住的数字是 5,000，以及 Karpathy 自己那句 —— 在这一层，人的审阅已经开始跟不上。

「跟不上」正是今天其余内容在回答的主题，而它们回答的是三个不同的对象。Thariq 谈的是模型：不要接受一个不透明的超级智能，因为沟通本身就是智能的信号。Micky（经 Peter Yang 转述）谈的是验收：前后都做证据驱动测试、greploop、由 agent 自己提交证据、人工审阅作为最后一步。Garry Tan 谈的是代码：把 LLM 调用换成确定性代码。同一片区域，三个对象，彼此没有引用，所以按 10-11 那条规则并列，不算互相印证。三个都没有数字。
