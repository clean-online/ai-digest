Madhu Guru Sr Director, AI at Meta

On evals, part 5 of his series: stop collapsing a rich, complex eval suite into a single score. It happens because some senior wants a simplified number to decide with — he saw it at Gemini's early days, and sees it across teams. His name for it: the "tyranny of the average." AI systems don't have one dimension of quality, and a single number hides where they actually fail.

关于 evals，这是他系列的第 5 篇：别再把一个丰富、复杂的评测套件压缩成一个分数。这么做往往是因为某位 senior 想要一个简化的数字来拍板——他在 Gemini 早期见过，如今在很多团队里也看到。他给这种现象起了个名字：“平均值的暴政（tyranny of the average）”。AI 系统的质量不是一个维度，单一数字会掩盖它真正失败的地方。

No Priors

What Chess.com Teaches US About Superhuman Capabilities, with CEO Erik Allebest

The Takeaway The No Priors team sits down with Chess.com CEO Erik Allebest to use chess as a 30-year live experiment in what happens to a human activity once machines become superhuman at it — and the surprising answer is that the game is more popular than ever.

The business. Allebest bought the chess.com domain out of a 2005 bankruptcy auction for $55 when nearly every investor called it an uninvestable niche. Two decades later it has 250M+ members, ~$200M/year in revenue, and a mission to get a billion people playing. COVID and Queen's Gambit spiked usage; each comedown settled at a permanently higher baseline.

The core question. Chess is "our longest-running experiment" in what happens after machines are superhuman. The non-obvious finding is that superhuman AI didn't kill the game — access to that intelligence makes people learn faster and better, and seven-year-olds are now giving grandmasters real runs.

The hard problem everyone now faces. Machines produce superhuman output that can pass as human performance. Chess.com defends with statistical ML models and a massive data moat — they know what human play looks like versus computer play. Allebest won't detail the defense, but says they track far more than users realize.

Skills in an abundant world. He argues human skill stays relevant even when AI is superhuman; people will simply spend more time on games. They're building an AI "chess coach in your pocket" with LLMs — a symbiosis, not a replacement.

Rating as truth. Bringing the chess playbook to poker — "how good are you really?" — because a real rating people care about as much as money reflects actual skill, not who rebuys the most chips.

核心结论 No Priors 请来 Chess.com CEO Erik Allebest，把国际象棋当作一个“机器变得超人之后，人类活动会怎样”的 30 年现场实验——而出人意料的答案是：这个项目比以往任何时候都更火。

生意。Allebest 在 2005 年一次破产拍卖里用 55 美元买下 chess.com 域名，当时几乎每个投资人都说这是个没人投得起的窄众项目。二十年后它有 2.5 亿+ 会员、约 2 亿美元年营收，使命是让十亿人会下棋。COVID 和《后翼弃兵》带来爆发，而每次回落都稳在一个更高的永久性基线。

核心问题。国际象棋是“我们运行最久的一个实验”，关于机器变超人之后会发生什么。反直觉的结论是：超人 AI 没有杀死这个项目——能接触到那种智能，反而让人们学得更快更好，如今七岁小孩已经能跟特级大师真正过招。

所有人都面对的难题。机器能产生以假乱真的超人输出。Chess.com 用统计机器学习模型和庞大的数据护城河来防守——他们清楚人类下棋和电脑下棋分别长什么样。Allebest 不愿细说防御手段，但表示他们追踪的东西远多于用户想象。

丰裕世界里的技能。他认为即便 AI 超人，人类技能依然相关，人们只会把更多时间花在游戏上。他们正用 LLM 打造“口袋里的国际象棋教练”——是共生，不是替代。

用评级逼近真相。把国际象棋的那套搬到扑克上——“你到底有多强？”——因为一个人们像在乎钱一样在乎的真实评级，反映的才是真本事，而不是谁反复买入的筹码最多。
