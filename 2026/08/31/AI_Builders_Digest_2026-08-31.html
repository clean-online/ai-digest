No Priors

From Restoring Sight to Reimagining the Brain, with Max Hodak

The Takeaway Max Hodak (founder & CEO of Science, formerly Neuralink) makes the case that the brain is literally a computer, and that treating it as one — rather than as biology to be chemically reverse-engineered — is what unlocked Prima, a retinal prosthesis that just won European approval and restores form vision to the blind. The deeper bet: AI and neuroscience are converging on shared representations (the "platonic representation hypothesis"), and that convergence is the on-ramp to substrate independence.

The device. Prima is a tiny chip implanted under the retina plus glasses with a laser projector; it bypasses dead photoreceptors and stimulates the retina directly. Think "cochlear implant for the eye." It's approved in Europe as of July 2026, with first commercial sales imminent. Clinical patients have filled in sudoku and read books — an existence proof that was previously impossible.

Why brain-as-computer. Hodak argues drugs fail because biology is a random walk; the brain, treated as a computer, gives you clear levers (you can turn a quadriplegic's motor cortex on and have them play a video game within an hour). Prima today is a proof of concept: black-and-white, straw-like field of view, but a clear engineering path to red/green and beyond.

The platonic representation hypothesis. Looking inside large models, the geometry of how they represent concepts mirrors neuroscience. Science uses this constructively — aligning animal neural recordings with AI internal representations. To Hodak this was the clue that AI wasn't a gimmick but grabbing onto a real "data manifold," almost a law of physics: enough compute on matter yields something like intelligence.

Pipeline & timeline. Three prongs — vision, biohybrid neural interfaces, and perfusion (project Vessel) — that together could drive a medicine revolution in 10–15 years. Connectomics is the missing piece; a mouse connectome is close, a human one still far.

Identity & substrate independence. A long detour into consciousness: a software copy of you that answers exactly like you isn't phenomenally continuous, and most people wouldn't find that satisfying. Continuity of experience matters more than static IQ. The real prize is upgrading/replacing parts of ourselves — attacking cardiovascular disease and brain-metastasized cancer, and eventually adapting bodies for space.

On "brain keyboards." Hodak is unimpressed by the popular BCI-as-high-bandwidth-AI-interface framing; he thinks talking/writing is thinking, and the 10-bits-per-second cognitive bottleneck rolls up through language anyway. Science targets restoring vision/hearing/motor and substrate independence, not a neural chat client.

核心结论 Max Hodak（Science 创始人，前 Neuralink）的核心理念是：大脑本质上就是一台计算机；正是这种"把它当计算机而非待化学逆向的生物"的视角，催生了 Prima——一款刚获欧洲批准、能为盲人恢复"形状视觉"的视网膜假体。更深的赌注在于：AI 与神经科学正在收敛到同一套表征（"柏拉图式表征假说"），而这条收敛线，正是通向"基底独立性（substrate independence）"的入口。

设备本身。Prima 是在视网膜下植入的一枚微小芯片，外加一副带激光投影的眼镜；它绕过失效的光感受器，直接刺激视网膜。可以类比"眼睛用的人工耳蜗"。它已于 2026 年 7 月在欧洲获批，首批商业销售在即。临床试验中，有患者完成了数独、读了书——这是此前被认为不可能的"存在性证明"。

为什么"大脑即计算机"。Hodak 认为药物的失败在于生物学的随机游走；而把大脑当计算机，你会得到清晰的杠杆（植入四肢瘫痪者的运动皮层，一小时内就能让他打电子游戏）。今天的 Prima 只是概念验证：黑白、视野像透过吸管看，但通往红/绿及更远是一条清晰的工程路径。

柏拉图式表征假说。窥探大模型内部，它们表征概念时的几何结构，与神经科学中看到的惊人相似。Science 把它用在了实处——让动物神经记录与 AI 内部表征对齐。对 Hodak 而言，这正是 AI 不是噱头、而是抓住了真实"数据流形"的线索，近乎一条物理定律：往物质上堆足够算力，就会涌现出类似智能的东西。

管线与时间线。三条主线——视觉、生物混合神经接口、灌注（Vessel 项目）——合在一起，可能在 10–15 年内推动一场医学革命。缺失的一环是连接组学；小鼠连接组已近在咫尺，人脑连接组仍远。

身份与基底独立性。一段关于意识的漫长岔路：一个回答方式与你完全一致的软件副本，并不具备现象学上的连续性，多数人大概不会觉得那"满足"。体验的连续性比静态的智商更重要。真正的奖品是升级/替换我们自身的部件——攻克心血管疾病与脑转移癌症，最终让身体适配太空。

关于"脑键盘"。Hodak 对把 BCI 当成"高带宽 AI 接口"的流行叙事兴趣不大；他认为说与写本身就是思考，而每秒约 10 bit 的认知瓶颈本就经由语言层层叠加。Science 瞄准的是恢复视觉/听觉/运动与基底独立性，而不是一个神经聊天客户端。
