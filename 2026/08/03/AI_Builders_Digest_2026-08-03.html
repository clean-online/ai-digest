AI Builders Digest

Today splits into two arguments about where AI's value actually lands. Levie says deep domains — math, science, legal, coding — are about to go vertical while daily life barely notices; Dan Shipper adds that this creates work for human experts rather than removing it; Nikunj Kothari points at the gap — models solving NP-hard problems while enterprises still argue about token ROI — and concludes that diffusion is the work of the next few decades. On the podcast, Netic founder Melisa Tokmak shows what that diffusion actually costs: the last mile of real-world AI comes from harnesses, orchestration and product, not from models.

今天的内容分成关于「AI 的价值到底落在哪」的两条论证。Levie 认为数学、科学、法律、编程这些纵深领域即将垂直起飞，而日常生活几乎察觉不到；Dan Shipper 补了一句——这反而给人类专家创造了更多工作，而不是减少。Nikunj Kothari 指出了那道裂缝：模型都在解 NP 难问题了，企业还在争论 token 花得值不值，他的结论是「接下来几十年要做的全是扩散和落地」。播客里，Netic 创始人 Melisa Tokmak 展示了这种扩散的真实代价：真实世界 AI 的最后一公里来自 harness、编排和产品，而不是模型。

Andrej Karpathy AI Researcher

Karpathy says we are leaving the era where you probe an LLM with a one-liner like "draw an SVG of a pelican on a bicycle." His generalization: hand Opus 5 the first paragraph of The Lord of the Rings, a 1M-token budget (~$10), and ask for a three.js render. The model ran for roughly two hours and produced 5,500 lines of code that procedurally rendered the scene. He calls the output janky but fun — and finds it genuinely mindboggling that the model has to place and orchestrate polygon assets in (x, y, z) space and write the animation code around them.

Karpathy 说，用一句「画一只骑自行车的鹈鹕 SVG」来测模型的时代正在过去。他给出的泛化版本是：把《指环王》的第一段丢给 Opus 5，给 100 万 token 预算（约 10 美元），要求用 three.js 渲染出来。模型跑了大约两个小时，写出 5500 行代码，程序化地把那段文字渲染成了场景。他形容结果「粗糙但好玩」，但真正让他觉得离谱的是：模型得自己在 (x, y, z) 空间里摆放和编排多边形素材，还要把动画代码一并写出来。

Aaron Levie Box CEO

Levie predicts a widening divergence between what AI does for personal life and everyday productivity versus what it can do in deep domains — math, science, legal, coding. Up to some threshold, capability was felt evenly across domains because models were only mildly useful in general. Now the deep-domain work is about to go vertical. Most people will not notice these gains directly in daily life, but the experts in those fields will — and he sees no inherent ceiling on the capability curve there.

Levie 预判 AI 的能力将出现明显分叉：一边是个人生活和日常办公，另一边是数学、科学、法律、编程这类纵深领域。在某个阈值之前，各领域感受到的能力提升是均匀的——因为模型对谁都只是「略微有用」。而现在，纵深领域的能力正准备垂直起飞。大多数人在日常生活里不会直接察觉到这波增益，但这些领域里的专家会；而且他认为这条能力曲线目前看不到天花板。

Nan Yu Linear Head of Product

Linear's Nan Yu proposes a concrete fix for the slop-PR problem: let people pledge tokens against issues they open on open-source repos. Write a spec in the issue with a pledge attached; if the maintainer accepts, GitHub passes the issue verbatim to a cloud coding agent at the requester's expense. The economics flip — the person who wants the work funds the compute, and maintainers stop drowning in unsolicited AI-generated PRs. He also described how the loop handles ambiguity in practice: the agent leaves a comment on the issue with all its context attached, and when you reply with the missing details, it simply picks up and keeps working.

Linear 的 Nan Yu 给「垃圾 PR」问题提了一个很具体的解法：让人在开 issue 时抵押 token。你在 issue 里写清 spec 并附上抵押额；维护者接受后，GitHub 就把这个 issue 原样交给云端 coding agent，算力费用由发起人承担。这就把经济关系反转了——想让活儿被干的人出钱，维护者也不必再被一堆没人要的 AI PR 淹没。他还讲了这个循环在遇到信息不足时的实际处理方式：agent 会带着完整上下文在 issue 下留言，你把缺的细节补上，它就直接接着往下干。

Guillermo Rauch Vercel CEO

Rauch highlighted an open-source agentic CRM built on Next.js — model-agnostic, self-hostable or serverlessly deployed, multi-channel, and headless. His verdict: "This is the way." The pattern he keeps endorsing is the same one: agentic software should be portable across models and deployment targets rather than locked to one vendor's stack. On a lighter note, he shared that when his three-year-old was asked at school what daddy does for work, the answer was "he exercises" — that is what the kid sees. His point: you are not the only byproduct of your habits; your children and grandchildren are too.

Rauch 转发了一个基于 Next.js 构建的开源 agentic CRM——模型无关、可自托管也可 serverless 部署、多渠道、headless。他的评价是「这才是正确路线」。他反复认可的其实是同一个模式：agentic 软件应该在模型和部署环境之间可迁移，而不是被锁死在某一家的技术栈上。另一条更轻松：学校问他三岁的儿子「爸爸是做什么工作的」，孩子回答「他健身」——因为那是孩子看到的。他的感慨是：你的习惯塑造的不只是你自己，还有你的子女和孙辈。

Garry Tan Y Combinator President & CEO

Garry Tan calls the most interesting vibe shift of 2026 the fact that OpenAI now looks like the open platform. He frames the split sharply: selling intelligence on tap as a utility versus signaling that the optimal move is to integrate all the way up the full stack. The strategic positions of the major labs, in other words, have quietly swapped from where most people expected them to land.

Garry Tan 认为 2026 年最有意思的风向变化，是 OpenAI 现在反而更像那个开放平台。他把这个分野讲得很清楚：一边是把智能当水电一样按需供给，另一边则是在暗示「最优解是把整个技术栈从下到上全都自己做」。换句话说，几家主要实验室的战略站位，已经和大多数人当初的预期悄悄对调了。

Separately he flagged a plugins bug at OpenAI that is breaking the UX for his /no-ai-slop skill.

Peter Yang 说了一个不太讨好的观点：Opus 4.6 才是所有 Opus 版本里性格和文风最好的一个。他觉得 Opus 5 有点不对劲——回复过长、「Claude 腔」太重（他举的例子是「说句实话」这种口头禅），语气还带着点评判感。他那句总结把损失讲得很准：以前跟 Opus 聊天像跟一个可信赖的朋友说话，是种享受；现在不太是了。另外他还反馈了 OpenAI 插件的一个 bug，正在影响他 /no-ai-slop skill 的使用体验。

Building an Autonomous Enterprise for Real-World Services with Netic Founder Melisa Tokmak

TakeawayNetic founder Melisa Tokmak on why the last mile of real-world AI cannot come from models alone — it has to come from harnesses, orchestration, and product. Over 70% of her customers are now AI-first, and the platform has generated $600M+ in revenue for them through AI-handled interactions.
核心观点Netic 创始人 Melisa Tokmak 讲清了一件事：真实世界 AI 的「最后一公里」不可能只靠模型，必须来自 harness、编排和产品。她的客户里超过 70% 已经是「AI 优先」，平台迄今通过 AI 处理的交互为客户创造了超过 6 亿美元的收入。

Melisa Tokmak founded Netic to run the operational layer of real-world service businesses — HVAC, plumbing, electrical, pet care, hospitality, automotive, consumer wellness. These are often billion-dollar-revenue enterprises, frequently private-equity owned, and Netic sits between the company and its millions of end customers: understanding the need, matching it against the operational rules of the business, and dispatching the labor.

The complexity is the point. When your heat dies at minus 20, the routing question is not simply "send someone." It is what unit do you have, can we service it, does it need to be today or tomorrow, what is your lifetime value as a customer, and should we deploy the one technician who can handle boilers now or later. Before Netic, that judgment was carried by hundreds of people per company — and it broke constantly. Tokmak describes the 4am reality: three people quit that day, five did not show up, and a heat wave is piling calls in from 6am.

Her answer to "can the labs just do this?" is the most quotable part. She notes the question ten years ago was "can Google do this?" She respects the leading labs and calls them good partners, but argues focus cuts differently in enterprise: OpenAI ships amazing products fast and kills them fast, which is not what these buyers want. And the researcher instinct — solve the most generalizable version, then ask the AGI how to handle essential services — she calls both operationally and intellectually lazy. Serving millions of people with different accents, contexts, and worries requires last-mile work that does not come from models. Companies like hers, she says, have to be good at all three layers.

On hiring, she screens for agency demonstrated continuously over a lifetime, not agency claimed in an interview. Her one question is what is the hardest thing you have ever done — and she digs into the why. She is explicitly wary of "shiny object seekers," and pushes back on what she sees as a Gen Z mentality organized around the permanent-underclass fear: if I don't make my money in 18 months I am poor forever. She calls that a dangerous mindset, because building good things takes a very long time.

The counterintuitive finding: these industries are not old school. A $500K contract closed end to end in fourteen days. A roofing customer still sends door knockers neighborhood to neighborhood, but Netic feeds satellite data on hurricane damage by neighborhood into agent context — so the company both handles the inbound conversation better and knows which streets to target. Her framing: these businesses are primal and tech-forward at the same time.

On private equity, she says the playbook has already changed — the undiscovered gem with a great multiple no longer exists, so the game is creating tangible value in what you own. Her complaint is that the first conversation is still always about cost cutting. Her pitch is the opposite: net new revenue. Her closing line on that: it would be pretty sad if we used AI only for cost cutting.

Melisa Tokmak 创办 Netic，做的是真实世界服务型企业的运营层——暖通空调、水管、电工、宠物护理、酒店、汽车、消费健康。这些常常是年收入十亿美元级别的大企业，很多由私募股权持有。Netic 站在公司和它数百万终端客户之间：理解需求、对照企业的运营规则做匹配、再把人派出去。

复杂性恰恰是关键。零下 20 度暖气坏了，调度问题从来不是「派个人过去」这么简单，而是：你家是什么机组、我们能不能修、是今天必须到还是明天也行、你这个客户的终身价值是多少、那位唯一会修锅炉的师傅该现在派还是待会儿派。在 Netic 之前，这些判断由每家公司几百号人扛着，而且经常崩。Tokmak 描述的凌晨四点是这样的：那天有三个人辞职、五个人没来，热浪来了，早上六点起电话就开始堆积。

她回答「实验室自己做不就行了吗」的部分最值得引用。她说十年前这个问题叫「Google 自己做不就行了吗」。她尊重头部实验室，也认为它们是很好的合作方，但她指出「专注」这件事在企业市场是反过来的：OpenAI 出好产品很快，砍产品也很快，而这不是这些买家要的。至于研究者的本能——先解最泛化的那版问题，等有了 AGI 再去问它怎么做基础服务业——她的评价是「运营上和智识上都偷懒」。要服务几百万口音不同、处境不同、顾虑不同的人，那段最后一公里不可能从模型里长出来。她说像 Netic 这样的公司，三层都得做好。

谈到招人，她筛的是在整个人生里持续展现出来的能动性，而不是面试时嘴上说的。她只问一个问题：你这辈子做过最难的事是什么——然后一路追问「为什么」。她明确警惕「追新鲜感的人」，也直接反驳了她观察到的一种 Z 世代心态，那种被「永久底层阶级」恐惧组织起来的心态：如果我 18 个月里没挣到钱，我就永远穷了。她认为这种想法很危险，因为把一件事做好，本来就需要很长时间。

一个反直觉的发现是：这些行业一点都不「传统守旧」。一份 50 万美元的合同，从头到尾十四天签完。一家做屋顶的客户至今还派人挨家挨户敲门，但 Netic 会把按社区划分的飓风损毁卫星数据喂进 agent 的上下文——于是这家公司既能把上门对话谈得更好，也知道该去敲哪几条街。她的总结是：这些生意同时是原始的和技术前沿的。

谈到私募股权，她说打法早就变了——那种「还没被发现、估值倍数又好」的璞玉已经不存在了，所以现在的游戏是在手里的资产上做出实打实的价值。她的抱怨是：第一轮对话依然永远从降本开始。而她要谈的恰恰相反——净新增收入。她那句收尾是：如果 AI 最后只被用来砍成本，那就太可惜了。
